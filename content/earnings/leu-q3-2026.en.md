## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: First new centrifuges ship two months early as US centrifuge manufacturing becomes reality</h3>
<p>Centrifuge manufacturing is complete, and shipments from Oak Ridge, Tennessee, to Piketon, Ohio, have begun ahead of the year-end target; key supplier contracts and the first certified-for-construction drawings were also completed early. CEO Amir Vexler said the milestone was "reached two months ahead of our ambitious schedule," and stressed on the call: "We now proved that U.S. centrifuge manufacturing is a reality."</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: EPS of $1.41 is an accounting figure; the adjusted loss is $0.46 per share</h3>
<p>CFO Todd Tinelli explained that the quarter included "a $46.4 million book gain" from a decline in the fair value of the warrant liabilities attached to the September offering. Excluding it, the adjusted net loss was $9.4M, versus adjusted net income of $5.2M a year earlier. The gross loss widened to $10.6M, SWU unit costs rose 28% YoY and the technical solutions segment swung from profit to loss, a clear weakening in core profitability.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: About $500M raised in September; full warrant exercise could bring in about $2B more</h3>
<p>Tinelli said the company completed an "approximately $500 million underwritten public offering" of common stock and pre-funded warrants, accompanied by common warrants with exercise prices of about $227 to $363; full exercise would bring in about $2B more, but only if the stock rises sharply (it is around $105 now, more than 100% out of the money). On 11/02 the company also filed an S-3 shelf registration. Vexler said the raise "keeps us well capitalized," but $220M of warrant liabilities will make future quarterly results swing widely with the share price.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: DOE's 2027 budget stops funding the HALEU line, putting $800M of backlog at risk</h3>
<p>DOE's proposed fiscal 2027 budget does not include further operating funds for the HALEU line, and DOE said it does not currently plan to exercise further options on the HALEU operations contract, which accounts for about $800M of the technical solutions segment's backlog. Centrus has switched its existing 16-centrifuge cascade to enriching HALEU for the commercial market and is negotiating the Piketon lease with DOE; Vexler said the goal is "a more wholesome agreement."</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $116M acquisition of Westerman secures centrifuge casing supply</h3>
<p>Tinelli said that after the quarter the company acquired Westerman for about $116M ($95M in cash plus $21M in stock), an amount not included in 2026 guidance. Vexler called it a "best-in-class manufacturer" of centrifuge casings and key components and said the deal "helps to derisk our build out." The Piketon hiring target was also raised from 100 to at least 175 people, and an investor day will be held on 12/07.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and 2026 Guidance

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: the only US enrichment capacity using its own technology + an unobligated supply chain</h3>
<p>Centrus is the only company enriching LEU and HALEU with domestic US centrifuge technology. Vexler stressed that its supply chain is "unobligated," allowing it to serve both national security and commercial markets. The $5.1B of signed contingent commitments provide a demand guarantee for the Piketon expansion.</p>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Piketon lease</b>: If a long-term lease cannot be agreed with DOE, the expansion could be delayed, scaled back or abandoned, and large asset impairments could be recorded.</li>
<li><b>HALEU contract funding cut</b>: DOE's 2027 budget no longer funds the HALEU line, so about $800M of technical solutions backlog may not be realized.</li>
<li><b>Core business in the red</b>: Gross loss of $10.6M, adjusted loss of $0.46 per share, and 9M operating cash flow has turned to an outflow.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Russian supply restrictions</b>: Guidance assumes Russian LEU import restrictions stay unchanged; if the ban or waiver terms change, SWU deliveries would be hit.</li>
<li><b>Warrant-driven earnings volatility</b>: $220M of warrant liabilities are remeasured with the share price, so future GAAP EPS could swing widely.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Ample funding</b>: $2.24B in cash and about $1.06B of net cash; full warrant exercise could raise about $2B more (only if the stock rises sharply).</li>
<li><b>First mover in commercial HALEU</b>: It is already enriching HALEU for the commercial market on its existing line and stands to benefit first as advanced reactor fuel demand takes shape.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Distorted EPS**: GAAP net income of $31.6M came entirely from the warrant fair-value gain; the adjusted loss was $0.46 per share.
- **Expansion ahead of schedule**: New centrifuges shipped two months early, and supplier contracts and construction drawings were all completed early.
- **DOE shift**: The 2027 budget stops funding the HALEU line; the $800M backlog and Piketon lease are the biggest variables.
- **Funding in place**: About $500M raised in September, $2.24B in cash, and full warrant exercise could bring in about $2B more.
- **Q4 deliveries decide guidance**: Full-year revenue guidance is $450–500M, requiring at least $142M in Q4.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Centrus's expansion continues, but core operations swung to a loss this quarter and DOE funding is winding down; the investment case now rests entirely on whether Piketon expands on schedule and on the lease negotiations.
