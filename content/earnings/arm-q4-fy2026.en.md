## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Record Revenue, Over 20% Growth for Three Straight Years</h3>
<p>Q4 revenue was $1.49B, up 20% YoY and above the $1.47B guidance midpoint; FY26 revenue was $4.92B, up 23% YoY. CEO Rene Haas noted <b>this was the third straight year of revenue growth above 20%</b>. Licensing revenue rose 29% YoY to $819M, driven by demand for Arm IP and the timing of several high-value license deals; royalties rose 11% YoY.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Data Center Royalties Double</h3>
<p>The shareholder letter said data center royalties more than doubled YoY, and Arm accounts for about 50% of CPU compute at the leading cloud providers. Google's new TPU generation uses the Arm-based Axion as its host processor, NVIDIA launched the Arm-based Vera CPU, and AWS's in-house chip business runs at an annualized rate of over $20B. Neoverse CSS is lifting both price per core and core counts.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: AGI CPU Demand Above $2B</h3>
<p>The Arm AGI CPU, Arm's first in-house production chip, targets agentic AI, with Meta as the main partner. Haas said demand <b>has exceeded the company's original expectations</b>: customer demand for FY27–FY28 is above $2B, more than double the level at launch, as Arm works toward a $15B business target. The company estimates agentic AI will raise CPU demand per GW of data center capacity 4x, with a market of over $100B by 2030.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Heavier R&D Squeezes Margins</h3>
<p>Non-GAAP operating expenses were $734M, up 30% YoY, and R&D rose 33% YoY, mainly from more engineering headcount; non-GAAP operating margin fell from 52.8% to 49.1%. As Arm shifts from pure IP licensing to making its own chips, its R&D and operating cost structure will change, and gross margin may fall below the 98% of its IP business (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Q1 Guidance Falls QoQ, Stock Drops 10%</h3>
<p>FY27 Q1 guidance calls for revenue of $1.26B ± $50M and non-GAAP EPS of $0.40 ± $0.04, well below Q4's $1.49B and $0.60, while operating expenses rise to about $760M. Licensing revenue timing is lumpy, and Q4's high licensing may lead to a Q1 pullback (our inference). On the first day after earnings the stock fell from $237.30 to $213.31.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and FY27 Q1 Guidance

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: the world's largest compute ecosystem</h3>
<p>More than 350B chips based on the Arm architecture have shipped, with over 22M developers, and it is used in phones, PCs, cars and data centers; the licensing-plus-royalty model has gross margins near 98%, and Armv9 and CSS raise the royalty per chip. The weak spots: licensing revenue timing is lumpy, and moving into its own chips may put Arm in competition with some licensees (our inference).</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Record revenue**: FY26 Q4 revenue of $1.49B (+20%), above the guidance midpoint; full-year FY26 revenue of $4.92B (+23%).
- **Strong licensing**: licensing revenue of $819M (+29%), royalties of $671M (+11%), and data center royalties doubled.
- **AGI CPU**: over $2B in customer demand for its first in-house chip, as it works toward a $15B business target.
- **Falling margins**: heavier R&D cut non-GAAP operating margin from 52.8% to 49.1%.
- **Weaker guidance**: FY27 Q1 guidance of $1.26B in revenue and $0.40 EPS; the stock fell 10% on day one.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Arm's FY26 Q4 revenue hit a record, data center royalties doubled, and demand for its in-house AGI CPU beat expectations; but Q1 guidance drops sharply QoQ, heavier R&D is squeezing profits, and a P/E above 100x leaves the stock very sensitive to short-term swings.
