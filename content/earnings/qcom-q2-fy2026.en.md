## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue In Line, EPS at the Top of Guidance</h3>
<p>Q2 revenue was $10.6B, down 3% YoY, at the midpoint of the $10.2–11.0B guidance; non-GAAP EPS of $2.65 hit the top of guidance. President and CEO Cristiano Amon said the results <b>were in line with guidance, reflecting solid execution in a challenging memory environment</b>. Handset revenue fell 13% YoY, mainly because memory shortages and price increases hurt demand at some handset customers.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Record Automotive, Diversification Paying Off</h3>
<p>QCT automotive revenue was $1.33B, up 38% YoY, a quarterly record; IoT was $1.73B, up 9%. Together, automotive and IoT grew 20% YoY and rose from 27% to 34% of QCT revenue (our estimate). With handsets weak, the diversified businesses held up revenue and reduced reliance on a single market, which is the focus of the company's long-term transformation.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Custom Data Center Chip Ships by Year-End</h3>
<p>Amon said the custom data center chip developed with a large hyperscaler <b>is on track to begin initial shipments later this year</b>, and that the rise of AI agents is reshaping the product roadmap for every platform. The company will update its growth plans, including data center and physical AI, at its 6/24 investor day. Alphawave, consolidated this quarter, sits in the data center business.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: New $20B Buyback Authorization</h3>
<p>Qualcomm completed $5.4B of buybacks in the first half, returned $3.7B to shareholders in Q2, and announced a new $20B buyback authorization. The jump in GAAP EPS to $6.88 came from reversing a $5.7B valuation allowance on deferred tax assets, a one-time item; non-GAAP net income actually fell 10% YoY. The share count fell from 1.115B to 1.072B in a year.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Lower Q3 Guidance, China Handsets Bottoming</h3>
<p>Q3 guidance calls for revenue of $9.2–10.0B, QCT revenue of $7.9–8.5B and non-GAAP EPS of $2.10–2.30, reflecting the impact of memory shortages and price increases on several handset customers. The company expects handset revenue from Chinese customers to bottom in Q3 and return to QoQ growth in Q4. Despite the weaker guidance, the stock rose from $156.00 to $179.58 on the first day after earnings.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q3 Guidance

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core Moat: Snapdragon Platform + Patent Licensing</h3>
<p>Qualcomm leads high-end Android phone chips with Snapdragon, QTL patent licensing earns a pre-tax margin above 70%, automotive and IoT have become a second growth curve, and the Alphawave acquisition takes it into data centers. The weak spot: handsets are still over 60% of revenue, leaving it highly sensitive to memory supply, Apple's in-house modems and demand from Chinese customers (our inference).</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **In line with guidance**: FY26 Q2 revenue of $10.6B (-3%) at the guidance midpoint, and non-GAAP EPS of $2.65 at the top end.
- **Record automotive**: automotive $1.33B (+38%), and automotive plus IoT up 20% YoY.
- **Weak handsets**: handsets $6.02B (-13%), hit by memory shortages.
- **One-time gain**: GAAP EPS of $6.88 includes a $5.7B tax benefit.
- **Outlook**: Q3 revenue guidance of $9.2–10.0B, the custom data center chip ships by year-end, and a new $20B buyback.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Qualcomm's FY26 Q2 came in line with guidance despite memory shortages, with record automotive, a data center chip about to ship and a $20B buyback for support; handsets won't bottom until Q3, so the stock's jump reflects hopes for new growth engines.
