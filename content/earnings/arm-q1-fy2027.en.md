## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Record quarter, EPS above the top of guidance</h3>
<p>Q1 revenue was $1.289B, up 22% YoY, with licensing and royalties both at first-quarter records; Non-GAAP EPS of $0.45 was above the top of the $0.40 ± $0.04 guidance. The shareholder letter said the record quarter reflects "growing demand for the Arm compute platform as <b>AI expands across cloud infrastructure, edge devices and the physical world</b>."</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: AGI CPU demand doubles to over $2B</h3>
<p>Arm launched its own AGI CPU chip in March, has shipped it to several customers, and secured capacity to support a $1B opportunity across FY27–28. CEO Rene Haas said: <b>"Demand for the Arm AGI CPU has continued to exceed our initial expectations,"</b> and the letter put customer demand at more than $2B across FY27–28, with new customers including U.S. and Chinese companies.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Data centers shift to Arm faster</h3>
<p>Data center royalties more than doubled YoY, cumulative Neoverse shipments passed 1.5B cores, and the latest 500M took only 9 months. NVIDIA's Vera CPU is in full production, Google Axion is the host CPU for TPUs, and AWS Graviton signed a multi-year deployment deal with Meta.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: AI extends to PCs and physical AI</h3>
<p>NVIDIA introduced its first agentic AI PC based on Arm CSS, "RTX Spark," with Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP and Lenovo launching systems by year-end. In physical AI, NVIDIA Jetson Thor combines an Arm CPU with a Blackwell GPU for humanoid robots. The shareholder letter said: <b>"As AI moves beyond the data center into billions of intelligent machines, Arm's compute platform is the CPU foundation for physical AI."</b></p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Q2 revenue guided to $1.38B</h3>
<p>Q2 revenue is guided to $1.38B ± $50M, Non-GAAP operating expenses to about $780M, and Non-GAAP EPS to $0.47 ± $0.04. Q1 Non-GAAP operating expenses were $733M, below guidance of about $760M, and R&D rose 20% YoY, mainly from investment in engineering headcount.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q2 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: the world's largest compute ecosystem</h3>
<p>The Arm architecture runs nearly every smartphone in the world, with more than 22M developers. Customers can choose IP licenses, CSS subsystems or buy the AGI CPU outright, all built on the same software ecosystem. The very high cost of switching architectures lets Arm charge higher royalties as chip value rises.</p>
</div>

<li><b>Competing with customers</b>: Its own AGI CPU may compete directly with licensees' chips.</li>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Record quarter**: FY27 Q1 revenue of $1.289B (+22%), with royalties and licensing each up more than 20% YoY.
- **Data center doubles**: Data center royalties more than doubled YoY, and Neoverse shipments accelerated.
- **In-house chip**: AGI CPU demand over FY27–28 exceeds $2B, twice the original estimate.
- **EPS beat**: Non-GAAP EPS of $0.45 above the top of guidance, with a 41.2% operating margin.
- **Strong guidance**: Q2 revenue guided to $1.38B and EPS to $0.47.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: In FY27 Q1 Arm's data center royalties doubled and demand for its own AGI CPU doubled, as it shifts from IP licensor to AI compute platform; but with a P/E above 200x, any slowdown in growth will amplify share price swings.
