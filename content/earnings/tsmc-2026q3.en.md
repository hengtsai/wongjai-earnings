## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: A remarkable 2nm ramp (3%➔8%) dilutes gross margin by only 1.3pp + an unusual new 3nm fab in Tainan</h3>
<p>In its second quarter of volume production, N2's share more than doubled from 3% to <b>8% (about $3.74B in the quarter)</b>, yet gross margin slipped only 1.3pp to 66.4%, confirming an excellent yield learning curve. CEO C.C. Wei said on the call that ramping small smartphone chips and large HPC chips at the same time is TSMC's advantage, and expects <b>annual capacity for the 2nm family to double in 2027</b>. With multi-year demand overflowing, the company also broke with convention to build <b>an additional 3nm fab in the Tainan park (volume production expected in H1 2028)</b>, while keeping the capital discipline of adopting High-NA EUV only around 2030 to protect gross margins above 65%.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: C.C. Wei confirms for the first time a team evaluating a Texas fab and talks on "Terafab," holding firm on operating control</h3>
<p>Asked by JPMorgan about rumors of a Texas fab and Elon Musk's Terafab plan, C.C. Wei publicly confirmed for the first time that, beyond pulling Arizona Fab 2's 3nm production forward to 2027 Q3, <b>the company has indeed sent a team to Texas to evaluate building a fab</b> and is discussing cooperation and capacity use with the customer planning <b>Terafab</b>. But he drew a firm red line: whether self-built, a joint venture or any other model, <b>"It must be operated by TSMC"</b>, preventing technology leakage and keeping pricing control.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Dispelling rumors of US power-driven order cuts — teams sent to check US grid and sites + a strict ban on customers reselling capacity</h3>
<p>Addressing market worries that US grid bottlenecks would trigger AI chip order cuts, C.C. Wei described an internal three-layer demand verification process: ① checking wafer starts with chip companies and <b>expressly forbidding customers from subleasing, reselling or auctioning TSMC capacity allocations</b>; ② checking 3–5 year capex and ROI directly with major North American CSPs; ③ <b>sending specialist teams to the US to inspect data center construction progress and grid power on site</b>. The teams reported that power and infrastructure are in good shape, no customer has lowered its forecast, and the earlier estimate of a mid-to-high 50% CAGR for AI accelerators over 2024–2029 "may still be too conservative."</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: CoWoS moves to 9X–14X reticle sizes + GlobalFoundries to make US silicon interposers</h3>
<p>Responding to Morgan Stanley's question about very large packages and competition, C.C. Wei stressed that TSMC's <b>5x reticle-size (5X) CoWoS is in volume production with excellent yields, and no one else in the market can do it today</b>; the company is pushing hard on 9X and moving toward 14X and panel-level packaging (CoPoS). To ease tight back-end capacity, TSMC confirmed <b>an agreement with GlobalFoundries to produce silicon interposers in the US</b>, handing mature-node interposers to a partner while focusing its own valuable cleanrooms on high-margin N2/N3 and high-end final packaging.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Q2 inventory build questions resolved — both turnover metrics improve and quarterly FCF hits a huge NT$476.6B</h3>
<p>In our Q2 report we flagged that receivables (+20.9%) and inventory (+23.8%) were growing faster than revenue. Q3 data confirm that was purely strategic stocking ahead of N2/CoWoS shipments: Q3 revenue rose 17.6% QoQ while receivables rose only 9.7% (<b>DSO down to 28 days</b>) and inventory only 11.0% (<b>DIO down sharply from 87 to 82 days</b>). Quarterly operating cash flow reached NT$919.8B, and after NT$443.2B of capex, quarterly free cash flow still jumped 242% YoY to <b>NT$476.6B ($14.9B)</b>, funding global expansion entirely in-house with zero equity dilution.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and 2026 Q4 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Moat rating: technology monopoly + economies of scale + ecosystem switching costs (10 / 10)</h3>
<p>TSMC has an effective monopoly in ≤5nm advanced nodes and CoWoS: A16 (Super Power Rail backside power delivery) targets high-power AI accelerators, and customer engagements for A14 (1.4nm, volume production in 2028) exceed those for N2 at the same stage. Asked about overseas fab costs and pricing, management confirmed strategic value-based pricing, saying <b>wafers made in the US will be priced somewhat differently from those made in Taiwan</b>, successfully passing on overseas costs; C.C. Wei put it boldly: <b>"Profitability has no ceiling."</b></p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh">
<ul>
<li><b>Wider long-term gross margin dilution from overseas fabs (3–4pp)</b>: The CFO warned that as Arizona Fab 2 (volume production in 2027 Q3), Japan Fab 2 (2028) and new fabs in Europe and the US come online, overseas dilution will widen from nearly 2pp in 2026 to <b>3–4 percentage points</b> in coming years, testing the ability to pass on a US wafer premium.</li>
<li><b>Depreciation pressure from a super capex cycle</b>: 2026 capex is as high as $60–64B, and spending over 2026–2028 is expected to be well above the previous three years; if AI compute build-out enters a digestion phase after 2028, heavy depreciation would weigh on profits.</li>
<li><b>Geopolitical and regulatory concentration risk</b>: North American customers account for <b>79%</b> and the most advanced nodes are concentrated in Taiwan, so US semiconductor tariffs or antitrust scrutiny remain external tail risks.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm">
<ul>
<li><b>Structurally higher effective tax rate</b>: The Q3 effective tax rate rose from 11.0% in Q2 to <b>16.2%</b>; as profits grow and operations in higher-tax overseas regions increase, the normal rate will stay in the 15%–17% range.</li>
<li><b>NT dollar volatility</b>: Nearly 100% of revenue is in US dollars, and the company estimates <b>each 1% appreciation of the NT dollar directly cuts gross margin by about 0.4 percentage points</b>.</li>
<li><b>Green power and facilities bottlenecks</b>: With nearly 20 wafer and packaging fabs under way worldwide, Taiwan's green power, water resources and CoWoS tool lead times face an extreme test.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl">
<ul>
<li><b>AI demand spreading from GPUs to every product line</b>: The boom in agentic AI inference is driving a surge in x86/Arm server CPUs, CSP in-house ASICs and silicon photonics, reducing single-customer concentration.</li>
<li><b>Nearly NT$3T of net cash leaves room for dividend increases</b>: Cash tops NT$4T, net cash is NT$2.99T and quarterly FCF is NT$476.6B, so future quarterly cash dividends (currently NT$24 a year) have room to keep rising.</li>
</ul>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

<div class="sb rl">
<ul>
<li><b>Record revenue and profit across the board</b>: Q3 revenue of NT$1.494T ($46.72B, above the top of guidance), operating margin of 58.7% and EPS of NT$29.43 (YoY +68.7%); nine-month EPS of NT$78.75 already exceeds full-year 2025.</li>
<li><b>Rapid 2nm ramp + a new 3nm fab in Tainan</b>: 2nm's share doubled to 8% in its second quarter while gross margin held at 66.4%, and N2 capacity will double in 2027; the company also broke with convention to build a new 3nm fab in Tainan (volume production in 2028H1).</li>
<li><b>Assets top NT$10T as turnover improves across the board</b>: Total assets topped NT$10.05T for the first time, cash exceeded NT$4T (net cash NT$2.99T), DSO fell to 28 days and DIO to 82 days, and quarterly FCF hit a record NT$476.6B.</li>
<li><b>C.C. Wei's three strategic messages</b>: ① confirmed a team evaluating a Texas fab and talks on Terafab, while insisting it "must be operated by TSMC"; ② sent teams to inspect the US grid on site, confirming no power-driven order cuts; ③ CoWoS moving to 9X–14X, with GlobalFoundries making silicon interposers in the US.</li>
<li><b>Full-year growth raised a third time to +44%, with a margin of safety in valuation</b>: Q4 revenue guidance is $52.3–54.3B (~14% QoQ), and the Taiwan share price of NT$2,240 implies a 2026E P/E of only ~19.8x; next, watch how well US wafer premiums offset 3–4pp of overseas fab dilution.</li>
