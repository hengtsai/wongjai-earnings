## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue Up 30%, Gross Margin Turns Negative</h3>
<p>Q3 revenue rose 30% YoY to $74.9M, driven by $34.1M in uranium sales; however, a 69% YoY drop in average SWU prices resulted in a $7.8M gross loss for LEU and a $4.3M company-wide gross loss. Management noted that LEU contracts are mostly annual commitments, making quarterly gross profit dependent on delivery mix—which skewed toward lower-priced legacy contracts in Q3 (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Operating Loss Offset by Interest and Tax Benefits</h3>
<p>Operating loss widened to $16.6M from $7.6M a year ago; $12.9M in investment income and an $11.9M tax benefit swung net income to $3.9M, or $0.19 diluted EPS. With $1.6B in cash generating over ten million dollars per quarter in interest, non-operating income should continue cushioning earnings during plant construction (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Russian Uranium Waivers Secured Through 2027</h3>
<p>Centrus received U.S. government import waivers for committed Russian deliveries in 2026 and 2027, reducing near-term supply risk. President and CEO Amir Vexler noted that with LEU SWU prices near historic highs, the market is showing <b>clear and accelerating demand for new U.S.-owned enrichment supply</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: $800M Zero-Coupon Convertible Offering Funds Expansion Prep</h3>
<p>In August, Centrus issued $805M in upsized, oversubscribed zero-coupon convertible notes, lifting cash to $1.63B. The company signed agreements with South Korea's KHNP and POSCO International to explore investing in its Piketon, Ohio enrichment expansion and began hiring. Vexler said the company <b>has made important preparations for enrichment expansion</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $3.9B Backlog Extending to 2040</h3>
<p>Total backlog stood at $3.9B, including ~$3.0B in LEU and ~$900M in Technical Solutions. The LEU backlog includes ~$2.3B in contingent commitments conditioned on securing substantial public and private investment for Piketon expansion, of which $2.1B is under definitive contracts. Visibility is long, though conversion depends on construction financing (our inference).</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core Moat: U.S.-Owned Centrifuge Tech & HALEU Line</h3>
<p>Centrus operates the DOE's HALEU demonstration cascade at Piketon, holds U.S.-origin centrifuge technology, and maintains long-term LEU customers with non-Russian supply options—giving it strategic value amid U.S. de-Russification and advanced reactor policies. Its weakness is that current LEU revenue relies largely on reselling third-party SWU, leaving margins sensitive to contract mix and import policy.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue growth**: Q3 revenue rose 30% to $74.9M, with $34.1M in uranium sales and a 69% YoY drop in SWU revenue.
- **Negative gross profit**: Lower SWU pricing led to a $7.8M gross loss in LEU and a $4.3M total gross loss.
- **Non-operating profit lift**: Investment income and tax benefits yielded $3.9M net income and $0.19 diluted EPS.
- **Capital raised**: $805M zero-coupon convertible issuance lifted cash to $1.63B.
- **Expansion prep**: Secured Russian supply waivers, signed deals with KHNP and POSCO, and holds $3.9B backlog.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Centrus posted a negative gross margin and operating loss in Q3 due to annual contract delivery mix, but with $1.6B in cash and a $3.9B backlog, the real test is securing financing and executing the Piketon expansion on schedule.
