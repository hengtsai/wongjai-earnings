## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Ad Volume and Pricing Both Rise, Driving 26% YoY Revenue Growth</h3>
<p>Revenue climbed 26% YoY (25% constant currency) to $51.24B, with ad impressions up 14% and average price per ad up 10%. Family daily active people reached 3.54B (+8% YoY). CEO Mark Zuckerberg noted that <b>it was a strong quarter for both the business and the community</b>, adding that Meta Superintelligence Labs is <b>off to a strong start</b> and that Meta continues to lead the industry in AI glasses.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: $15.9B One-Time Tax Charge Masking Lower Future Cash Taxes</h3>
<p>New tax legislation significantly reduces Meta's U.S. federal cash taxes for the rest of 2025 and beyond, but the Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax required a valuation allowance on deferred tax assets, triggering a $15.93B one-time non-cash tax charge. GAAP EPS fell 83% YoY to $1.05; excluding the charge, EPS rose 20% YoY to $7.25 with an effective tax rate of 14%. Q4 tax rate is guided at 12–15%.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Expenses Up 32% YoY, Operating Margin Compresses to 40%</h3>
<p>Total costs and expenses rose 32% YoY to $30.71B, outpacing 26% revenue growth, with R&D up 35.5% and G&A up 88%. Operating margin contracted from 42.7% to 40.1%, while Family of Apps operating margin fell from 54.0% to 49.2%. Full-year expense guidance was narrowed to $116–118B (+22–24% YoY), raising the low end from $114B.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: 2026 Capex and Expenses Both Set to Accelerate Materially</h3>
<p>2025 capex guidance was raised from $66–72B to $70–72B. The CFO noted that compute needs <b>continued to expand meaningfully compared to last quarter's expectations</b>, prompting Meta to build out its own infrastructure and contract with third-party cloud providers, and added that <b>2026 capex dollar growth will be notably larger than 2025</b>, while total expense growth will also accelerate significantly—driven primarily by infrastructure and cloud costs plus depreciation, followed by AI talent compensation.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Free Cash Flow Drops 32% YoY as Cash Shrinks $33.4B in Nine Months</h3>
<p>Q3 capex including leases surged 111% YoY to $19.37B, pulling free cash flow down 32% YoY to $10.63B. Cash and marketable securities fell from $77.8B to $44.4B, while share buybacks slowed from $8.8B a year ago to $3.3B. The CFO also warned that EU regulators could require changes to Meta's less personalized ads offering, potentially resulting in a significant negative impact on European revenue as early as this quarter.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Strong Ad Growth**: Revenue of $51.2B (+26%) on +14% impressions and +10% average price, with 3.54B daily active people.
- **Tax Charge Distorts EPS**: One-time non-cash tax charge of $15.93B left GAAP EPS at $1.05, versus $7.25 (+20%) excluding it.
- **Accelerating Expenses**: Total costs and expenses rose 32% YoY, compressing operating margin to 40.1%.
- **2026 Step-Up**: Capex dollar growth will exceed 2025, with expense growth accelerating notably.
- **Weaker Cash Flow**: Free cash flow fell 32% YoY to $10.6B, with cash and marketable securities down $33.4B over nine months.

> 💡 **Bottom Line**: Meta's ad engine kept humming at 26% growth, but guidance for markedly faster capex and expense growth in 2026 sparked questions over AI payback timelines, sending the stock down 11% on day one.
