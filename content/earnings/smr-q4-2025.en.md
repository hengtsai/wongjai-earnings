## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: The only SMR with NRC design approval</h3>
<p>CEO John Hopkins said <b>2025 was a breakthrough year that further cemented NuScale's position as the first mover in the SMR industry</b>. NuScale remains the only SMR technology with design approval from the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, including the uprated 77 MWe NuScale Power Module. Each module is 77 MWe, and up to 12 modules can be combined for 924 MWe.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: ENTRA1 and TVA up to 6 GW, but still non-binding</h3>
<p>Exclusive global commercialization partner ENTRA1 reached a non-binding collaboration agreement with the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) to deploy up to 6 GW of NuScale SMRs across TVA's seven-state service area, which the press release calls the largest nuclear deployment program in US history. But the agreement is non-binding and has not yet turned into power purchase or equipment contracts, so the timing of any revenue contribution is unclear.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: $507M milestone expense drives up losses</h3>
<p>G&A expense rose from $75.9M to $610M, mainly because of the $507M ENTRA1 milestone contribution recognized under the partner milestone agreement. The full-year operating loss was $690M, the net loss attributable to common stock $356M, or $2.17 per share. Excluding this expense, the operating loss was about $180M, about $43M more than 2024's $139M (our estimate).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Revenue depends on a single project; only $1.81M in Q4</h3>
<p>Full-year revenue was $31.48M, down 15% YoY, mainly because of lower revenue from the RoPower technology license signed in 2024; FEED Phase 2 engineering services performed by Fluor for Romania's RoPower plant increased but wrapped up at the end of 2025. Q4 revenue was only $1.81M, down 95% YoY. Until the first modules are manufactured, revenue lacks a stable source.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $750M raised; 2026 focused on first-module manufacturing</h3>
<p>In Q4 the company sold 39.3M shares through its ATM program, raising $750M, and ended the year with $1.3B in cash and investments. Hopkins said the main focus for 2026 is <b>commercializing its SMR technology</b>, including ensuring manufacturing readiness for the first modules. The company also published a study showing that modules can supply process steam and power to chemical plants, opening up industrial uses.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: first-mover advantage from NRC design approval</h3>
<p>NuScale is the only SMR with US Nuclear Regulatory Commission design approval, a threshold other SMR makers will still need years to reach. Its modular design can combine 1 to 12 modules for the grid, data centers and industrial process heat. But approval is only the first step; the real moat can only be validated once the first modules are delivered and a plant is running.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Shrinking revenue**: Full-year revenue of $31.48M (-15%), only $1.81M in Q4, dependent on the single RoPower project.
- **One-time expense**: The $507M ENTRA1 milestone contribution widened the full-year operating loss to $690M.
- **Regulatory lead**: The only SMR with NRC design approval, including the 77 MWe model.
- **Orders not firm**: ENTRA1 and TVA's up-to-6 GW plan is still a non-binding agreement.
- **Running on equity raises**: $750M raised in Q4, $1.3B in cash and investments, and $460M of operating cash burned during the year.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: NuScale holds the only NRC design approval and a TVA cooperation intent for up to 6 GW, but until firm orders appear, revenue is near zero and it runs on equity raises; the valuation rests entirely on whether commercialization delivers.
