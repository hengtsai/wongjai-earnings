## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue falls, still only a few million in scale</h3>
<p>Q4 revenue was $1.87M, down 18% YoY, and full-year revenue $7.09M, down 34%; the press release does not break down revenue sources. CEO Subodh Kulkarni said <b>the company expects significant YoY revenue growth in Q1</b>, coming from previously announced Novera on-premises system orders totaling about $5.7M, part of which is expected to ship in Q1.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: National-level customers buy complete on-premises systems</h3>
<p>India's C-DAC ordered a 108-qubit chiplet system worth about $8.4M, expected to be deployed in 2H 2026 and integrated into a high-performance computing environment. Kulkarni said <b>demand for on-premises quantum systems from governments and research institutions continues to grow</b>, as national-level customers want direct access to quantum hardware. A Japanese research institution also ordered a Novera QPU, the company's first processor located in Japan.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Fidelity closing in on other technology approaches</h3>
<p>The prototype platform's two-qubit gate fidelity reached up to 99.9% with a 28-nanosecond gate time; the 9-qubit and 36-qubit systems have medians of 99.7% and 99.6%, and the 108-qubit Cepheus-1-108Q is at 99%. The company says superconducting technology is about 1,000x faster than trapped ions and neutral atoms. Kulkarni said the next step is <b>deploying a 108-qubit system with 99.5% median fidelity</b>, on the way to practical quantum advantage.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Chiplets are the path to more qubits</h3>
<p>Kulkarni said <b>a chiplet-based approach is a practical and scalable path to large-scale quantum systems</b>. Tiling small chips keeps chips uniform and improves yields as qubit counts grow; the in-house Fab-1 allows rapid iteration across design, fabrication and testing, and partners include Riverlane (error correction), NVIDIA, Quanta and QphoX.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $590M on hand, burning about $77M</h3>
<p>Full-year operating cash flow was -$58.5M and capex $18.7M, for free cash flow of about -$77M; year-end cash and investments of $590M can last more than 7 years at this burn rate (our estimate). Q4 interest income of $5.77M was more than 3x quarterly revenue. Funding came mainly from the ATM program and warrant exercises, and the share count rose 17% in a year.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: vertical integration and an in-house fab</h3>
<p>Rigetti does everything in-house, from chip design and Fab-1 fabrication to control electronics and cloud services, and its chiplet tiling is one of the few commercially available multi-chip quantum architectures. The weakness is scale: full-year revenue was just $7.09M, mostly from government and research orders, and commercialization is still far off.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Shrinking revenue**: Q4 revenue of $1.87M (-18%) and full-year $7.09M (-34%); the CEO says Q1 will grow significantly YoY.
- **National-level order**: C-DAC's roughly $8.4M 108-qubit system, expected to deploy in 2H 2026.
- **Fidelity gains**: Up to 99.9% on the prototype; the 108-qubit Cepheus-1-108Q is at 99% with a 99.5% target.
- **Wider losses**: Operating loss of $22.6M, widening every quarter; the narrower GAAP net loss reflects a smaller warrant valuation effect.
- **Ample funding**: Cash and investments of $590M, no debt, annual burn of about $77M.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Rigetti kept improving its technical metrics in 2025 and won a national-level complete-system order, but full-year revenue fell to $7.09M; $590M in cash gives it years to prove its technology, while a price-to-sales ratio near 800x already prices in long-term success.
