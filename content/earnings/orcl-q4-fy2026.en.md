## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: OCI Revenue Up 93% YoY</h3>
<p>Q4 revenue was $19.18B, up 21% YoY and at the top of guidance; cloud revenue was $9.91B, up 47% YoY, including OCI infrastructure at $5.8B (+93% YoY) and SaaS at $4.1B (+10% YoY). Software revenue fell 2% YoY, which the company attributed to customers continuing to move from on-premises to the cloud. The press release described Oracle as <b>the world's fastest-growing cloud data center provider</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: RPO Up $85B QoQ</h3>
<p>Remaining performance obligations (RPO) rose from $553B to $638B, up 363% YoY. The company said most of the Q3 and Q4 increase came from large AI contracts in which customers prepay for GPUs or supply their own; these together already total $75B and, per the company, <b>substantially reduce the capital Oracle needs to raise to build AI data centers</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Free Cash Flow of -$23.7B</h3>
<p>FY26 operating cash flow was $32B, up 54% YoY, but capex was $55.7B, 2.6 times last year's, leaving free cash flow of -$23.7B. Total debt rose $37B to $129.5B, and interest expense rose 47% YoY. FY27 still requires about $40B in financing, including a $20B ATM equity program, so dilution and credit ratings are concerns (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: FY27 Revenue of $90B</h3>
<p>The company confirmed FY27 revenue guidance of $90B and raised Non-GAAP EPS guidance to $8.05 (18% growth after excluding one-time items in FY26 such as the sale of the Ampere chip business and Bloom Energy warrants). Q1 guidance calls for revenue growth of 27–29% YoY, cloud growth of 58–64% YoY, and Non-GAAP EPS of $1.72–1.76.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: AI Drives Database and Health</h3>
<p>Multicloud AI database grew 404% in Q4, the fastest-growing business in Oracle's history. The company said Oracle Health will soon launch an all-new AI version of its Cerner patient care system, which it expects to return the health business to double-digit growth in FY27. Oracle also plans to power data centers with natural-gas fuel cells. The stock fell 8.5% on the first day after earnings.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and FY27 Guidance

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: Database + AI cloud infrastructure</h3>
<p>Oracle has a huge installed base in enterprise databases and uses OCI to win large AI training and inference contracts; $638B in RPO provides multi-year revenue visibility, and customer GPU prepayments or customer-supplied GPUs ease part of the capital burden. The weaknesses: capex far exceeds operating cash flow, debt has reached $129.5B, and profits depend heavily on a handful of large AI customers (our inference).</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Cloud acceleration**: Q4 revenue of $19.18B (+21%), cloud at $9.91B (+47%), OCI up 93% YoY.
- **Orders soar**: RPO up $85B QoQ to $638B; customer prepayments and customer-supplied GPUs total $75B.
- **Earnings beat guidance**: Non-GAAP EPS of $2.11, above $1.96–2.00 guidance.
- **Capital-intensive**: FY26 capex of $55.7B, free cash flow of -$23.7B, total debt of $129.5B.
- **FY27 guidance**: Revenue of about $90B and Non-GAAP EPS raised to $8.05, with about $40B more financing needed.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: In FY26 Q4 Oracle's cloud accelerated and RPO surged to $638B, leaving no doubt about AI cloud demand; but $55.7B of capex produced -$23.7B of free cash flow and nearly $130B of debt, and the market has begun pricing in concerns about funding pressure.
