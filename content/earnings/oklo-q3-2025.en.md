## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Aurora-INL breaks ground</h3>
<p>The first Aurora plant broke ground at Idaho National Laboratory in September, and contractor Kiewit mobilized on 10/27 to start earthwork, with controlled blasting slated for mid-November and full excavation in January. During the quarter the company began procuring major long-lead equipment, including the fuel handling machine, sodium pumps, and reactor trip system. This marks Oklo's inflection from planning into construction (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Shift to the DOE authorization pathway</h3>
<p>Oklo and its subsidiary were selected for three projects under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program: Aurora-INL, the Atomic Alchemy isotope pilot plant, and the Pluto test reactor. The company shifted Aurora-INL onto the DOE authorization pathway to speed initial operation while pursuing commercial NRC licensing in parallel for subsequent plants. The presentation states this model can compress the timeline from pilot to commercial deployment.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Expanding the fuel footprint</h3>
<p>Oklo announced a Tennessee fuel campus and is evaluating a collaboration with TVA to recycle its used nuclear fuel and potentially sell power; the Nuclear Safety Design Agreement for the Aurora fuel fabrication facility received DOE approval and was selected for the DOE Fuel Line Pilot Program. It also partnered with Blykalla and newcleo, and is conducting fuel and materials testing with Battelle Energy Alliance.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Expenses triple, but cash burn remains in check</h3>
<p>R&D and G&A expenses both roughly tripled year over year, widening the operating loss to $36.3M, partially offset by $7.1M of interest income. Nine-month operating cash outflow was $48.7M, which the presentation says remains within the full-year estimate of $65–80M. Annualizing 9M free cash flow, the $1.18B cash balance could last well over a decade (our estimate), though plant construction spending will ramp rapidly (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Isotopes target 2026/7 operation</h3>
<p>The Atomic Alchemy pilot plant aims to go from greenfield to criticality within a year, targeting operation in July 2026; over the medium term it plans lab-scale production and early revenue, and long term a license for its first commercial-scale Versatile Isotope Production Reactor (VIPR). Isotopes could be Oklo's earliest revenue-generating business (our inference).</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: Vertical integration of power, fuel, and isotopes</h3>
<p>Oklo builds on fast-reactor technology demonstrated at EBR-II under a build-own-operate power-sales model, while expanding into fuel recycling, fuel fabrication, and isotopes. Three DOE pilot projects give it a chance to gain early operating experience. Weaknesses are the lack of revenue and commercial plants, with fuel cost and sourcing still uncertain.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **No revenue yet**: Q3 operating loss of $36.3M and net loss of $29.7M ($0.20 loss per share).
- **Construction underway**: Aurora-INL broke ground in September; Kiewit mobilized on 10/27 for earthwork.
- **DOE pilot program**: Selected for 3 projects; Aurora-INL shifts to DOE authorization to accelerate.
- **Fuel and isotopes**: Announced Tennessee fuel campus; Atomic Alchemy targets 2026/7 operation.
- **Well funded**: $527M net ATM proceeds, $1.18B in cash and securities, and cash burn within estimates.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: In Q3 Oklo moved from planning to construction, breaking ground on its first plant and winning three DOE pilot projects with ample cash; however, it still has zero revenue and an extreme valuation, so the next year hinges on whether its pilot projects reach criticality on schedule.
