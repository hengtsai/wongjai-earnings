## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: AI Liquid Cooling Hits Inflection Point, Advancing from Components to Full-Rack Integrated Solutions</h3>
<p>Management noted on the earnings call that per-rack power consumption for GB200 servers exceeds 120kW, pushing air cooling to its physical limit. Delta not only supplies cold plates and fans, but has also expanded into coolant distribution units (CDUs) and manifolds, breaking into NVIDIA and the top four CSP supply chains. Infrastructure Q4 revenue surged 32%, with gross margins above the group average, becoming the company's top profit engine.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: AI Power Wattage Leaps, Global Share in Server PSUs & HVDC Tops 50%</h3>
<p>Demand for high-end AI server power supply units (PSUs) has surged from 3.2kW to over 5.5kW, lifting both ASPs and gross margins. Delta holds over 50% global market share in server power supplies and is leading standard-setting for 800V high-voltage direct current (HVDC) architecture, making it hard for customers to qualify a second source and creating a formidable switching moat.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: P/E Surges from 20x to 65x as Market Re-Rates Delta as a Core Thermal Tech Play</h3>
<p>Over the past year, shares surged 470% from NT$275 to NT$1,565, lifting market cap past NT$3.9T. The P/E expansion to 65x reflects a complete re-rating from traditional electronics manufacturing to a core global AI infrastructure beneficiary—though such a rich multiple sets a demanding bar for earnings CAGR over the next two years.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: New Thailand Plant Capacity Comes Online, Overseas Localization Eases Geopolitical Concerns</h3>
<p>To meet North American CSP customers' supply chain resilience requirements, Delta Thailand's new plant has entered full production alongside expanded system assembly bases in Texas and Dallas. Non-cross-strait overseas capacity now exceeds 45%, mitigating tariff risks and ensuring on-time delivery of high-end thermal components—earning strong trust from U.S. cloud giants.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Industrial Automation Bottoming Out, Set for 2026 Equipment Replacement & Restocking Cycle</h3>
<p>Although the Automation segment posted a slight full-year decline, the Q4 contraction narrowed to -11.2%, with factory automation inquiries recovering in Europe and Southeast Asia. Management expects Automation to see sequential quarterly recovery in H1 2026 as semiconductor back-end packaging and lithium-battery expansion resume, serving as the group's next growth relay.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and 2026 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core Moat: Full-Stack Power Electronics Integration & Dominant Global Server Thermal Leadership</h3>
<p>Delta is uniquely capable worldwide of vertically integrating from grid input, power transformation, distribution, and uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) down to chip-level liquid cooling. In an era of surging AI GPU thermal design power, system stability is mission-critical, and steep qualification hurdles create a formidable moat against substitution.</p>
</div>

> 📌 Delta Electronics does not provide full-year quantitative financial guidance. Management expects the Automation segment to recover sequentially in H1 2026 (see insights above); unverified third-party estimates are not listed.

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>P/E at historical extreme of 65x</b>: Any slowdown in AI server CapEx or delay in liquid-cooling qualification would expose the rich multiple to sharp mean-reversion pressure.</li>
<li><b>Thermal peer price competition & qualification hurdles</b>: AVC, Auras, and U.S. thermal majors are accelerating into liquid cooling, risking margin erosion if price wars emerge.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Sluggish industrial automation recovery</b>: If global traditional manufacturing CapEx fails to rebound as expected, it will continue weighing on Automation operating margins.</li>
<li><b>Overseas expansion management costs & yield ramp</b>: Rising labor and local compliance costs at new plants in Thailand, the U.S., and Europe could temper near-term operating leverage.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Surge in data center microgrids & energy storage systems</b>: North American power shortages are driving enterprise self-generation demand, positioning Delta's solid-state transformers and microgrids for major orders.</li>
<li><b>Long-term growth in EV charging & powertrain systems</b>: With global charger standards settling, EV solutions are positioned to become the third major operating pillar over the long run.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **AI Liquid Cooling Establishes New Earnings Cycle**: Infrastructure revenue rose 32% YoY as CDU and cold-plate shipments ramped, lifting gross margin above 34% and underscoring a successful transformation.
- **Unmatched Dominance in High-End Server Power**: Doubling AI server wattage reinforces Delta's share lead, with HVDC and proprietary architectures forming steep competitive barriers.
- **Dual-Track Overseas Supply Chain Established**: Localized assembly in Thailand and the U.S. sharply lowers geopolitical risk, making Delta a preferred supplier for North American cloud customers.
- **Re-Rated Valuation at Historical Highs**: At 65x P/E and 14.6x P/B, optimism is well priced in; investors should closely track GB200 ramp progress and gross margin trends.
- **Cash-Cow Profile Funds Expansion**: Record quarterly OCF and full-year FCF allow 100% internal funding of CapEx with no leverage strain.

> 💡 **One-Line Takeaway**: Delta Electronics has successfully evolved from a traditional power supplier into an indispensable cornerstone of power and liquid cooling for global AI data centers, though a 65x P/E demands flawless high-growth execution in the quarters ahead.
