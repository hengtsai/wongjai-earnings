## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue down 73% QoQ; the full-year target rides almost entirely on C-DAC</h3>
<p>Q2 revenue was $5.14M and Q3 fell back to $1.41M; the 10-Q attributes the YoY decline mainly to lower collaborative research and professional services revenue. Full-year guidance of about $20M implies about $9.05M in Q4, while the 108-qubit system order from India's C-DAC received in January is $8.4M. CFO Jeff Bertelsen acknowledged the guidance <b>assumes customer acceptance of the C-DAC system in the fourth quarter</b>, and said revenue recognition is tied to a handful of system deliveries and <b>can swing widely from quarter to quarter</b>, recommending a year-to-date view.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: 108-qubit fidelity of 99.5%; the chiplet approach clears a key threshold</h3>
<p>Cepheus-1-108Q is built from 12 nine-qubit chiplets and reached a median two-qubit gate fidelity of 99.5%, with 99.6% on an 80-qubit subarray and 99.7% on a 50-qubit subarray. CEO Subodh Kulkarni said <b>the company does not believe scaling requires a trade-off among qubit count, fidelity and speed</b>. He noted that the only publicly deployed systems above 100 qubits are IBM's (120) and Google's (105), with Quantinuum at 98. In Q2 the company said coherence time of 25–30 microseconds was the main bottleneck for fidelity and is still being improved.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: New roadmap fits 1,000 qubits in a single refrigerator</h3>
<p>The next goal is a system with more than 300 qubits, about 99.7% fidelity and gate speeds under 60 nanoseconds by mid-2028, and about 1,000 qubits at 99.9% and under 50 nanoseconds within about 3 years. The approach is to raise qubit density inside a single dilution refrigerator rather than linking several, and chiplets will grow from 9 to 36 qubits. Kulkarni said that for error correction to be useful <b>you need at least 100 physical qubits and 99.7% or even 99.8% fidelity</b>, which the company has not yet reached.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: $100M Commerce Department award is booked as equity, not revenue</h3>
<p>In September the company signed a CHIPS R&D award of up to $100M with the US Department of Commerce, supporting three programs: miniaturizing readout electronics, a new cryogenic architecture and a high-connectivity chip process. Commerce receives a minority stake of about 7.74M shares, or about $12.9 per share. Bertelsen explained that funds are drawn monthly against actual spending and <b>are simply a debit to cash and a credit to equity</b>, so they will not flow through the income statement. Nothing had been drawn as of the end of September. Kulkarni added that some programs will be shared with partners and will bring capex the company would not otherwise have made.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Cash burn accelerates, and the CEO admits returns cannot yet be proven</h3>
<p>R&D expenses rose 70% YoY, non-GAAP net loss doubled to $20.5M, and nine-month capex was 3 times last year's. Bertelsen signaled that 2027 capex will be <b>roughly in line</b> with 2026. Asked when volumes would ramp, Kulkarni said plainly that quantum computing is still in the R&D stage: <b>none of the companies can yet prove a return on investment for quantum computing versus classical computing</b>. $497M in cash and no debt buy time, but the commercialization timeline is still measured in years.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat, Guidance and Roadmap

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: own fab + modular chiplets + superconducting gate speed</h3>
<p>Rigetti designs and manufactures its quantum chips in its own Fab-1, so it can test individual chiplets before integrating them into systems. The company says its superconducting gate speed of 50–70 nanoseconds is about 10,000 times faster than trapped ions and about 100 times faster than neutral atoms. The 108-qubit system is available on its own QCS, Amazon Braket, Microsoft Azure and qBraid; it is working with Riverlane on real-time error correction, and the 9-qubit Novera developed with HPE is expected to be installed at the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center in 2027.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>One contract decides the year</b>: About $9.05M of Q4 revenue depends almost entirely on C-DAC acceptance; if it slips to 2027, full-year revenue would be only a little over $11M.</li>
<li><b>Commercialization is far off</b>: The CEO admits returns cannot yet be proven; operating loss is 24x revenue, and the nine-month operating loss is $87.2M.</li>
<li><b>Extreme valuation</b>: Market cap is about $3.8B against TTM revenue of only $12.82M, a price/sales ratio of nearly 300x.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Cash burn and capex</b>: Q3 free cash flow was -$43.26M, 2027 capex stays elevated, and at the Q3 pace cash lasts about 11 quarters.</li>
<li><b>Equity dilution</b>: Commerce takes 7.74M shares, and there are another 8.43M public warrants (exercise price $11.50).</li>
<li><b>Technology competition</b>: IBM and Google have comparable qubit counts and far greater resources, and coherence time remains Rigetti's bottleneck.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Financial cushion</b>: $497M in cash and investments, no financial debt, plus up to $100M of Commerce Department funding.</li>
<li><b>Government relationships</b>: The US, UK and Indian governments are all customers or partners, about 80% of Q3 revenue came from government entities, and national security needs support orders.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Sharp sequential revenue drop**: Q3 revenue of $1.41M was down 73% QoQ, and the stock fell 8.6% the next day.
- **Technology milestone met**: The 108-qubit system reached 99.5% fidelity, and the new roadmap targets 300 qubits by mid-2028 and 1,000 qubits in about 3 years.
- **Full year hinges on C-DAC**: The $20M guidance requires about $9.05M in Q4, nearly equal to the $8.4M C-DAC order.
- **$100M Commerce stake**: In exchange for 7.74M shares; funding is recorded in equity rather than revenue and had not been drawn by end of September.
- **Deeper losses**: Non-GAAP net loss doubled to $20.5M; the GAAP net loss is small only because of the warrant fair-value gain.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Rigetti delivered 99.5% on the technology front and won government backing, but revenue still swings with a few system orders. At a $3.8B market cap, the market is buying the post-2028 roadmap, not today's business.
