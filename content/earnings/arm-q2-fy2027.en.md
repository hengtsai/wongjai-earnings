## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Licensing revenue soars 50% to $773M as Arm Total Access deals take off</h3>
<p>Licensing revenue hit a quarterly record, driven by a doubling of Arm Total Access (ATA) and Compute Subsystems (CSS) licenses. CEO Rene Haas noted that customers no longer buy a single CPU IP but adopt complete, pre-verified compute subsystems, cutting design cycles for their advanced chips to a matter of months.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: In-house AGI CPU could bring $2B over two years, ramping in Q4 FY27</h3>
<p>The biggest surprise of the call was Arm formally raising its target for its in-house AGI chip business: up to $2B of chip sales across FY2027 and FY2028. The first AGI CPU will start shipping to major cloud and sovereign AI customers in Q4 FY27, marking Arm's move from pure IP licensor to a supplier of higher-value physical compute solutions.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Hyperscale data center adoption tops 50%, cloud royalties double</h3>
<p>The cloud architecture shift keeps accelerating. AWS Graviton4, Google Axion and Microsoft Cobalt 100 already accounted for more than 50% of server deployments at the major CSPs in 2025, and are expected to exceed 60% by the end of 2026. The advantage in dense, power-efficient computing doubled data center royalty revenue YoY, making it the fastest-growing royalty driver.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Quarterly chip shipments top 10 billion, Armv9's higher rate keeps paying off</h3>
<p>Partners worldwide shipped 10 billion Arm-based chips in the quarter (up 1.5 billion YoY). As smartphones and edge devices move faster to Armv9 (whose royalty rate is more than twice Armv8's), the average royalty per chip (ASP) keeps lifting total revenue even with flat consumer electronics shipments.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Arm PC share tops 20%, physical AI and edge robotics royalties surge 50%</h3>
<p>The ramp of Qualcomm's Snapdragon X series and upcoming MediaTek/NVIDIA AI PC chips lifted Arm's global PC share to 20%. In "physical AI" areas such as smart vehicles, industrial edge robots and unmanned vehicles, royalties grew as much as 50% YoY, building a broad moat at the edge.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q3 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: Dominance as the global architecture standard, ultra-low power and a diverse subsystem ecosystem</h3>
<p>99% of the world's smartphones, more than 50% of cloud servers and 20% of PCs use Arm, with more than 20 million developers in its ecosystem. As cloud compute power draw approaches grid limits, Arm's performance per watt has become an irreplaceable computing standard in the AI era.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>The open-source RISC-V ecosystem taking share at the edge</b>: If microcontroller and low-end IoT markets adopt open-source RISC-V faster, it could erode Arm's low-end IP licensing base over time.</li>
<li><b>In-house AGI chips may create conflicts of interest with customers</b>: Selling AGI CPUs directly may raise competitive concerns among some custom-chip customers, testing the balance of the business model.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Follow-up to the Qualcomm licensing lawsuit and patent disputes</b>: An adverse ruling in the core Nuvia dispute could affect the royalty basis and contract terms for some PC chips.</li>
<li><b>Longer smartphone replacement cycles</b>: Global handset shipment growth is slowing; if the Armv9 transition slows, near-term royalty upside will be limited.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Broader rollout of CSPs' in-house CPUs</b>: Microsoft, Google and Amazon's in-house chips are replacing x86 faster, sharply doubling royalty value per server.</li>
<li><b>AI PC adoption accelerating in enterprise and consumer markets</b>: The Windows on Arm ecosystem is maturing; if PC share heads toward 30% in 2027, it would bring a large royalty windfall.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Licensing momentum surges**: Licensing revenue jumped 50% to over $770M, with long-term ATA and CSS deals speeding up chip design starts.
- **In-house AGI chip breakthrough**: Expected to contribute $2B of revenue with deliveries from Q4 FY27, a major evolution of the business model.
- **Cloud server share tops 50%**: The major CSPs are shifting fully to Arm, keeping server royalties on a strong doubling trend.
- **Quarterly shipments top 10 billion**: Armv9 adoption lifts the average royalty rate, offsetting the consumer electronics slump.
- **PCs and physical AI both take off**: Arm PC share topped 20%, and automotive and edge robotics royalties grew a strong 50% YoY.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: With licensing up 50%, more than half the cloud market and an in-house AGI chip targeting $2B, Arm has transformed from a pure IP licensor into a full-scale AI computing solutions giant.
