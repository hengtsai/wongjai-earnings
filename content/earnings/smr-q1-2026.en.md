## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue Down to Just $570K</h3>
<p>Q1 revenue fell from $13.38M a year ago to $570K. The press release said last year's Q1 included technology licensing revenue from Romania's RoPower plus Fluor's phase 2 front-end engineering work, which was completed at the end of 2025, with no similar work this year. NuScale's revenue will not recover until it signs commercial plant contracts, leaving almost no revenue source in the near term.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: $260M Paid to ENTRA1</h3>
<p>This quarter NuScale paid $259.9M to its exclusive global partner ENTRA1, pushing operating cash flow to -$315M and cutting accounts payable from $287M to $21.6M. The payment was an expense recognized in 2025 under the partnership milestone agreement and paid in cash this quarter (our inference). NuScale has to pay its partner first to advance projects, a business model with a heavier cash burden than simply selling equipment.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: CEO Stresses It Is the Only One With NRC Approval</h3>
<p>President and CEO John Hopkins said NuScale is <b>the only SMR company with a US NRC-approved design, a mature supply chain, and module components already in production</b>. He said this quarter it expanded its supply chain partnership with Framatome, the TVA program continues to advance, and the company is <b>building the infrastructure this pivotal moment demands</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Romania Project Takes a Step Forward</h3>
<p>Shareholders of Romania's state nuclear company SN Nuclearelectrica approved moving the RoPower project to its next phase, which plans six NuScale modules at the former Doicești coal plant. ENTRA1 also continues to plan up to 6 GW of deployment with TVA, which the press release called potentially the largest nuclear deployment program in US history, though it remains a non-binding agreement.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Rising Expenses, Cash Topped Up via ATM</h3>
<p>R&D rose 40% YoY to $12.8M to advance the technology readiness of module components; other expenses doubled to $19.9M, and the operating loss widened to $57.5M. Investment income of $10.8M offset part of it. Quarter-end cash and investments were about $1B, and after the quarter the company raised another $217M via ATM, funding its burn through ongoing equity issuance.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Full-Year Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: The only NRC-certified SMR</h3>
<p>NuScale is the first and only SMR to receive US NRC design certification; its module components are already being produced by suppliers such as Doosan, and ENTRA1 handles commercialization and project development. That gives it an edge in fastest deployment; the weakness is that it still has no firm commercial plant contract, revenue is almost zero, and it must pay milestone payments to its partner.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue near zero**: Q1 revenue of $570K ($13.38M a year ago), as the Romania licensing and front-end engineering ended.
- **Payment to partner**: $260M paid to ENTRA1; operating cash flow of -$315M; cash and investments down to about $1B.
- **Wider losses**: R&D +40% and other expenses doubled; operating loss of $57.5M and net loss attributable of $44M.
- **Project progress**: RoPower shareholders approved the next phase, and Framatome expanded its fuel supply partnership.
- **Ongoing equity raises**: New ATM program of up to $1B, with another $217M raised after the quarter.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: NuScale had almost no revenue in Q1 and paid $260M to its partner ENTRA1; its regulatory and supply chain lead remains, but until TVA or RoPower turns into a firm order, the company must rely on ATM equity raises to fund its burn.
