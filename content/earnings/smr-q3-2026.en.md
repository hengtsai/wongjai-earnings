## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: From design to manufacturing, Doosan starts the most critical first component</h3>
<p>On 10/29 the company announced a contract with Doosan Enerbility to manufacture the first NPM "upper reactor cooling system" at its Changwon plant in South Korea, which the company calls the most complex and fundamental component of the module. Long-lead materials on the balance sheet (large forgings, steam generator tubing) have reached $69.7M. CEO John Hopkins said on the call: "We are no longer simply designing and planning, we are manufacturing."</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Build first, sell later; no customer order yet behind the first component</h3>
<p>TIKR notes that the Doosan component is not backed by any binding customer order, meaning NuScale is building inventory at its own expense. The upside is shorter lead times once a contract is signed; the risk is that if the TVA project is delayed, this inventory will sit on the books for a long time. Each module produces 77 MWe net, and the design has NRC standard design approval, making it the only SMR in the US with NRC design certification.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: TVA power purchase agreement still unsigned; market expects early 2027 at the earliest</h3>
<p>The ENTRA1–TVA deployment plan remains the top commercial priority, but for now there is only a non-binding agreement. Barclays expects negotiations to last until at least early 2027. CFO Hamady also cautioned that even a signed power purchase agreement (PPA) is not a module sale; modules require a separate contract with ENTRA1 before revenue comes in. That means the revenue gap could last through the first half of 2027.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: $495M one-time charge disappears; net loss narrows but revenue nearly vanishes</h3>
<p>In 2025 Q3 the company recognized a $495M ENTRA1 "Milestone 1 Contribution," pushing G&A up to $519M; this year Q3 G&A returned to $27.9M, and the net loss narrowed from $533M to $45.7M. But revenue also fell from $8.24M to $281K, mainly because the second-phase design work for Romania's RoPower (FEED Phase 2) was completed at the end of 2025 and no new project has followed.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $1.86B in firepower and only $45.6M of liabilities, at the cost of ongoing dilution</h3>
<p>Net equity raised over the first nine months was $985M, Class A shares rose from 318M to 411M (+29%), and a new ATM agreement signed on 8/11 allows up to $750M more in sales. At quarterly operating expenses of about $65M, TIKR estimates the cash lasts about 7 years. Hopkins said period-end liquidity is sufficient to support full-scale production, and the CFO expects operating expenses to stay roughly flat.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: the only NRC-certified SMR design in the US + an early start on the Korean supply chain</h3>
<p>NRC design certification takes years and is costly, and NuScale is the only SMR to have passed so far; with its partnership with Doosan, dating back to 2019, now moving into actual production, its supply chain is ahead of other SMR companies. But turning the moat into money still hinges on the first binding module order.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Longer revenue gap</b>: Q3 revenue was only $281K with no guidance; if the TVA project drags past 2027, revenue could stay near zero for several quarters.</li>
<li><b>Single-customer dependence</b>: Commercialization rides almost entirely on ENTRA1 and TVA; if the non-binding agreement changes, both inventory and valuation would come under pressure.</li>
<li><b>Shareholder litigation</b>: Media report that the 10-Q discloses three shareholder lawsuits concerning disclosures about ENTRA1's capabilities and commercialization; the outcomes are pending.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Ongoing equity dilution</b>: Class A shares rose 29% over the first nine months, and the ATM has up to $750M of capacity left, so per-share value keeps getting diluted.</li>
<li><b>Inventory before orders</b>: Doosan's first component has no customer order behind it, and $69.7M of long-lead materials risk sitting idle.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Very clean balance sheet</b>: $1.86B in cash and investments and only $45.6M of liabilities, so there is no near-term debt or liquidity pressure.</li>
<li><b>AI data centers short on power</b>: If large power demand prompts TVA or other utilities to sign, the NRC-certified design can be deployed fastest.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Year one of manufacturing**: Doosan has started the first upper reactor cooling system, moving NuScale from paper design into physical production.
- **Net loss back to normal**: Last year's $495M one-time ENTRA1 charge did not recur, and the quarterly net loss narrowed to $45.7M.
- **Revenue near zero**: No new project followed the end of the RoPower design work, and Q3 revenue was only $281K with no guidance.
- **TVA is the only big catalyst**: The market expects the power purchase agreement no earlier than early 2027, and module sales need a separate contract.
- **Market cap ≈ cash**: EV is only about $320M, as the market gives almost no premium for the technology or order outlook.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: NuScale has cash, the only certified design in the US and has started manufacturing, but until the first binding module order appears, the stock will simply swing with news about the TVA negotiations.
