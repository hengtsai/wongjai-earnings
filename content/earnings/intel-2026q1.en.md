## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue tops the company's own forecast for the sixth straight quarter</h3>
<p>Q1 revenue was $13.58B, $880M above the top of the $11.7–12.7B guidance given in January; non-GAAP gross margin of 41.0% was 6.5pp above the 34.5% guidance. CEO Lip-Bu Tan said <b>deliberate changes to how the company operates have driven revenue above its forecast for the sixth straight quarter</b>. CFO Zinsner had said in January that Q1 would be the tightest quarter for supply, yet revenue fell just 0.7% QoQ, mainly because the fabs squeezed out more usable capacity.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: DCAI operating margin doubles as CPUs regain weight in AI inference</h3>
<p>DCAI revenue was $5.05B, up 22% YoY, and operating income rose from $575M to $1.54B, taking operating margin from 13.9% to 30.5%. Tan said AI is moving from foundation models to inference and agents, <b>sharply increasing demand for Intel CPUs, wafers and advanced packaging</b>. This quarter Xeon 6 became the host CPU for NVIDIA DGX Rubin NVL8, and Google signed a multiyear deal to keep deploying Xeon and co-develop custom ASIC IPUs.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: The GAAP loss comes from two non-operating items</h3>
<p>The GAAP loss of $0.73 per share had two main causes: $4.07B of restructuring and other charges, which the company said was mainly a goodwill impairment at the Mobileye reporting unit (goodwill fell $3.45B in the quarter); and a $1.09B valuation loss on U.S. government escrowed shares, which grows as the stock price rises. Together they come to about $1.01 per share; after the non-controlling interest share, non-GAAP EPS was $0.29.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Foundry revenue up 20% QoQ, but losses not yet narrowing</h3>
<p>Intel Foundry revenue was $5.42B, up 20% QoQ, but most of it is internal revenue from Intel's own products (company-wide intersegment eliminations of $5.25B), and the operating loss of $2.44B was larger than last year's $2.32B. 18A began shipping in mainstream Core Series 3 processors this quarter; expanding packaging and test capacity in Penang and joining Terafab alongside SpaceX, xAI and Tesla mark progress with external customers.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Cash down $4.6B in a quarter, capex shared with partners</h3>
<p>Gross capex was $4.96B, down 20% YoY, with partner contributions of $1.96B, and adjusted free cash flow of -$2.02B improved from -$3.68B a year ago. Cash and short-term investments fell to $32.79B as the company repaid $1.5B of debt and bought back the 49% minority stake in the Ireland Fab 34 joint venture (amount not disclosed in the press release). Zinsner said Intel will keep <b>maximizing its factory network to increase available supply</b>.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q2 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: The x86 ecosystem plus U.S. domestic manufacturing</h3>
<p>Intel owns x86 CPU design, wafer manufacturing and advanced packaging, Xeon remains a common host CPU in AI servers, and partnerships with Google and NVIDIA extend the ecosystem's life. The U.S. government stake and CHIPS Act escrow arrangement make Intel the standard-bearer for leading-edge manufacturing on U.S. soil. The weak spot is foundry: external revenue is still small, and losses run above $2B a quarter.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Ongoing foundry losses</b>: Foundry lost $2.44B in the quarter, up 5% YoY, and whether 14A proceeds depends on external customer commitments.</li>
<li><b>Cash flow still negative</b>: Adjusted free cash flow of -$2B, with cash and short-term investments down $4.6B in one quarter.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Escrowed-share valuation swings</b>: A rising stock price amplifies valuation losses on government escrowed shares, making GAAP earnings more volatile.</li>
<li><b>Gross margin easing</b>: Q2 guidance calls for non-GAAP gross margin of 39.0%, 2pp below Q1.</li>
<li><b>Asset impairments</b>: Mobileye goodwill has already been impaired, and the remaining $20.47B of goodwill could still be written down.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>AI inference lifts CPUs</b>: DCAI up 22% YoY with operating margin doubled, and the Google and NVIDIA deals provide visibility.</li>
<li><b>Expenses keep falling</b>: R&D + MG&A down 8% YoY and headcount down 19%, as operating leverage starts to show.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue beat**: Q1 revenue $13.58B (+7%), $880M above the top of guidance, the sixth straight quarter above the company's forecast.
- **Data center strengthening**: DCAI up 22% YoY with a 30.5% operating margin, and Xeon 6 won slots in new NVIDIA and Google platforms.
- **GAAP loss is one-time**: The Mobileye goodwill impairment and escrowed-share loss total about $1 per share; non-GAAP EPS was $0.29.
- **Foundry is still a black hole**: Foundry lost $2.44B in the quarter, most revenue is internal, and external customer progress is still early.
- **Cash flow needs work**: Adjusted free cash flow of -$2B, and Q2 guidance has gross margin easing to 39%.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Intel showed its product side has stabilized, with revenue above its own forecast for a sixth straight quarter and DCAI operating margin doubling, but foundry losses and negative free cash flow show the turnaround is still early.
