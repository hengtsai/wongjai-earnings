## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue down 18% YoY and gross margin drops sharply</h3>
<p>Q3 revenue was $1.95M, down 18% YoY and slightly above Q2's $1.80M, while gross margin fell from 50.6% last year to 20.7%. Rigetti's revenue base is tiny—coming mainly from government research contracts and limited hardware sales—so quarterly results can swing sharply (our inference). R&D rose 18% YoY to $15.0M, widening the operating loss to $20.5M.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Warrant valuation pushes net loss past $200M</h3>
<p>GAAP net loss was $201M (-$0.62 per share), including $182M in non-cash warrant fair-value losses triggered by the surge in the stock price; non-GAAP net loss was only $10.7M (-$0.03 per share). Warrant liabilities rose from $93M at the start of the year to $241M, so share-price swings will continue to amplify reported GAAP earnings.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Growing demand for on-premises systems</h3>
<p>During the quarter Rigetti received orders for two on-premises Novera quantum computers totaling about $5.7M, slated for delivery in 1H 2026. CEO Subodh Kulkarni said the company saw <b>strong demand for on-premises quantum computers</b> in Q3 alongside expanding R&D collaborations. That order value alone exceeds quarterly revenue and should materially boost revenue upon delivery (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: 2027 1,000-qubit roadmap</h3>
<p>Kulkarni said the company <b>remains on track to launch a 100-plus-qubit chiplet system by the end of 2025</b> with a 99.5% median two-qubit fidelity. The newly unveiled roadmap targets 150-plus qubits (99.7%) around year-end 2026 and 1,000-plus qubits (99.8%) around year-end 2027. Fidelity is the key threshold for practical utility (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Left out of DARPA's initial Stage B cohort</h3>
<p>On 11/6, DARPA announced the initial Stage B cohort for its Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (QBI) without Rigetti; the company said it received constructive feedback from DARPA, remains in ongoing dialogue, and is optimistic about potential selection in the coming months. It also continues collaborations with AFRL, C-DAC, and Montana State University, and supports NVIDIA NVQLink.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: Chiplet architecture + in-house fab</h3>
<p>Rigetti designs and fabricates superconducting quantum chips at its own Fab-1 foundry, scaling systems by tiling chiplets with an open, modular architecture that facilitates integrating partner technologies like QphoX. Fast gate speeds are an advantage of superconducting qubits. Weaknesses are tiny revenue, fidelity that still needs improvement, and no guarantee of winning government program competitions.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue decline**: Q3 revenue of $1.95M (-18%), with gross margin falling to 20.7%.
- **Large paper loss**: $182M warrant fair-value loss pushed GAAP net loss to $201M; non-GAAP net loss was $10.7M.
- **New orders**: Two Novera systems for ~$5.7M and an AFRL quantum network contract for $5.8M.
- **Roadmap**: 100+ qubits (99.5%) by year-end 2025, 150+ in 2026, and 1,000+ in 2027.
- **Ample cash**: ~$600M in cash and investments with no debt, though valuation exceeds 1,000x sales.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Rigetti's Q3 revenue remained tiny and shrinking while warrant valuation magnified the GAAP loss; the 1,000-qubit roadmap and on-premises orders fuel the narrative, but missing DARPA's initial cohort and trading above 1,000x sales leave on-time technical execution as the sole support.
