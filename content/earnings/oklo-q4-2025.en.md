## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Zero revenue, Q4 loss widens to $57.1M</h3>
<p>Oklo is still not selling power; Q4 operating loss was $57.1M, 3.7x a year earlier. Full-year R&D expense rose 120% YoY and G&A 208%; the 10-K attributes this mainly to higher employee compensation and professional fees, and the official presentation adds that it includes capital-markets-related costs. Full-year stock-based compensation was $41.8M.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: From R&D to construction, capex ramps up</h3>
<p>The official presentation says that in 2025 Oklo <b>moved from product development to project deployment</b>: construction began on the first Aurora powerhouse at Idaho National Laboratory (INL), the DOE signed an OTA and approved a nuclear safety design agreement, and all major equipment is under supplier contracts, including the Siemens Energy power conversion system. Q4 capex was $27.0M, about 80% of the full-year $33.2M.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Meta prepayment deal advances 1.2GW Ohio campus</h3>
<p>On 2026/1/5 Oklo signed a prepayment agreement with Meta to advance a 1.2GW powerhouse campus in Pike County, Ohio, under which Meta prepays for power and the funds go toward procuring nuclear fuel. The presentation says the first 150MW phase targets around 2030, and subsequent power purchase agreements will be used to seek project financing from government energy financing agencies and commercial banks. The deal value was not disclosed.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Isotope business could contribute revenue as soon as this year</h3>
<p>The test reactor of acquired Atomic Alchemy in Groves, Texas, finished its building in 5 months, has its fuel procured and targets criticality on July 4. The Idaho radiochemistry lab has obtained an NRC materials license, and the presentation expects it to generate first revenue this year. On fuel, the A3F fuel fabrication facility received DOE approval of its preliminary safety analysis.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Plenty of cash, but burn is accelerating and the share count keeps rising</h3>
<p>Year-end cash and marketable securities were $1.41B; adding roughly $1.2B from the January ATM, funds on hand exceed $2.6B (our estimate). The 10-K says existing funds are sufficient for at least the next year of operations. But Q4 free cash flow alone was -$60.4M, about $240M annualized, and shares outstanding rose from 161M at year-end to 174M on 3/13.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and 2026 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: fuel flexibility and vertical integration</h3>
<p>Oklo's fast reactors can run on multiple fuels, including fresh HALEU, recycled fuel and surplus plutonium, and the company is building three business lines at once: power, fuel fabrication and recycling, and isotopes. Its first assets follow the DOE authorization pathway, which can speed construction; but commercial plants still need NRC licenses, and metal-fueled fast reactors lack commercial operating experience.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Still no revenue**: Q4 operating loss of $57.1M (vs. $15.4M a year earlier); full-year net loss of $106M.
- **Into the build phase**: Construction began on the first Aurora at INL; Q4 capex of $27.0M was about 80% of the full year.
- **Meta's backing**: A prepayment deal advances the 1.2GW Ohio campus, with the first 150MW targeted for around 2030.
- **Ample funding**: Year-end cash and marketable securities of $1.41B, another ~$1.2B raised via the January ATM, and no financial debt.
- **The price is dilution**: Shares outstanding rose 17% in a year, and Q4's annualized burn of about $240M keeps rising.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: In 2025 Oklo raised more than $1.2B in equity for ample funding, secured a Meta prepayment deal and progress on DOE authorization, and formally moved from R&D into construction; but revenue is still zero and burn is accelerating, so the stock hinges on whether the first plant and the isotope business deliver on schedule.
