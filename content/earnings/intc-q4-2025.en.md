## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue, Gross Margin, and EPS All Top Guidance</h3>
<p>CFO David Zinsner stated that <b>Q4 revenue, gross margin, and EPS all topped projections despite industry-wide supply shortages</b>. Revenue of $13.67B landed in the upper half of the $12.8–13.8B guidance range, while Non-GAAP EPS of $0.15 was $0.07 above the $0.08 guidance. Still, GAAP gross margin fell 3.1pp YoY to 36.1%, and GAAP EPS remained a loss of $0.12.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: First 18A Products Hit the Market</h3>
<p>CEO Lip-Bu Tan noted that <b>launching the first Intel 18A products—the most advanced process technology developed and manufactured in the U.S.—marks an important milestone</b>, adding that the company is actively expanding supply to meet strong demand. Core Ultra Series 3 is the first 18A AI PC platform. He also said his confidence in the critical role of CPUs in the AI era <b>continues to grow</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: DCAI Operating Margin Jumps from 9% to 26%</h3>
<p>DCAI revenue rose 9% YoY to $4.74B, with operating income climbing from $370M to $1.25B and operating margin expanding from 8.6% to 26.4%. Client Computing (CCG) revenue fell 7% YoY and operating margin dropped from 36.4% to 27.0% due to supply constraints and cost headwinds. Combined Intel Products operating margin was 26.8%, similar to last year's 27.2%.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Q1 Supply Hits Trough, Conservative Guidance</h3>
<p>Zinsner said <b>available supply is projected to hit its lowest point in Q1 before improving from Q2 onward</b>, while underlying demand fundamentals across core markets remain healthy. Q1 guidance calls for revenue of $11.7–12.7B (-11% QoQ at the midpoint), Non-GAAP gross margin of 34.5%, Non-GAAP EPS of $0.00, and GAAP EPS of -$0.21. The soft outlook drove a 17% drop on day one post-earnings.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Cash Up $15.4B, Foundry Losses Still Widening</h3>
<p>Cash and short-term investments increased by $15.4B YoY to $37.4B, debt fell by $3.4B, Intel stockholders' equity rose by $15.0B, and share count grew by 660M shares; full-year equity sales including Altera generated $5.3B in gains. Q4 adjusted FCF turned positive at $2.2B. However, Intel Foundry lost $2.5B in Q4 and $10.3B for the full year, remaining the biggest drag.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q1 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core Moat: x86 Ecosystem Plus U.S. Manufacturing</h3>
<p>Zinsner noted that rapid AI adoption reinforces x86's position as the world's most widely deployed high-performance computing architecture. Combining CPU design with domestic U.S. 18A leading-edge manufacturing gives Intel strategic value amid supply-chain localization demand, though its foundry business remains unprofitable and external customer scale has yet to materialize.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Above Guidance**: Q4 revenue of $13.67B and Non-GAAP EPS of $0.15 both topped company guidance.
- **18A Milestone**: First 18A products launched, with supply actively ramping.
- **Data Center Improvement**: DCAI operating margin rose from 8.6% to 26.4%, while CCG declined amid supply constraints.
- **Soft Q1**: Available supply drops to its trough, with revenue guided to $11.7–12.7B and Non-GAAP EPS breakeven.
- **Foundry Still Loss-Making**: Foundry lost $2.5B in Q4 and $10.3B for the year, while cash rose via the Altera sale and equity issuance.

> 💡 **Bottom Line**: Intel delivered a Q4 above guidance and launched its first 18A products, but warning of trough Q1 supply and breakeven EPS guidance sent the stock down 17%—a durable turnaround still hinges on 18A volume ramp and stemming foundry losses.
