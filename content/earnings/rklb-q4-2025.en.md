## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: $816M SDA contract doubles the backlog</h3>
<p>Backlog rose from $1.10B in Q3 to $1.85B, up 73% YoY, mainly on the Space Development Agency's (SDA) $816M contract for 18 Tracking Layer Tranche 3 satellites, the largest single contract in company history. CEO Peter Beck said this makes Rocket Lab <b>a disruptive new prime contractor of choice</b>, winning contracts that used to belong only to traditional aerospace primes.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Launch services up 79% YoY</h3>
<p>Q4 had 7 launches, a quarterly record; full-year Electron and HASTE launches totaled 21 with a 100% success rate, and more than 30 new launch contracts were signed in 2025. Launch services revenue was about $75.9M, up 79% YoY; space systems was about $104M, down 9% QoQ. Three HASTE hypersonic tests supported Defense Department priorities such as Golden Dome.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Record gross margin, but losses flat</h3>
<p>GAAP gross margin was 38.0%, up 10.2pp YoY, and the adjusted EBITDA loss of $17.4M was better than guidance. But R&D expense was $78.8M, up 63% YoY, mainly for Neutron, so the $51.0M operating loss was flat with last year. Q4 included $11.3M of income tax, for a net loss of $52.9M.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Neutron's first flight pushed to Q4</h3>
<p>Neutron's Hungry Hippo fairing has completed qualification and been shipped to the Virginia assembly facility, and the thrust structure has also passed qualification; but after a stage-one tank test failure, the company moved the first flight to 2026 Q4. Neutron is key to Rocket Lab entering medium-lift launch and competing with SpaceX for constellation deployment, and the delay means continued R&D spending and later revenue contribution (our inference).</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Q1 guidance up 57% YoY</h3>
<p>Q1 guidance calls for revenue of $185–200M, up 57% YoY at the midpoint, GAAP gross margin of 34–36%, an adjusted EBITDA loss of $21–27M and a share count of 605M. The gross margin guidance is about 3pp below Q4, and the EBITDA loss is wider than Q4, so growth still comes with losses (our estimate). In Q4 the company also acquired Optical Support and Precision Components, strengthening optical payloads and precision manufacturing.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and Q1 Guidance

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: vertical integration of launch and satellites</h3>
<p>Electron is the world's most frequently launched small orbital rocket, and Rocket Lab also makes its own satellite buses, solar panels and components, letting it take on whole-satellite SDA contracts as a prime contractor. The weakness is Neutron: the medium-lift rocket has yet to fly, its schedule keeps slipping, and it requires continued capital investment.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Record revenue**: Q4 revenue of $180M (+36%) at the top of guidance; full year $602M (+38%).
- **Backlog doubles**: Backlog of $1.85B (+73%), with the $816M SDA satellite contract the largest single award in its history.
- **Launch record**: 21 launches for the year with a 100% success rate; launch services revenue up about 79% YoY.
- **Better margins**: GAAP gross margin of 38.0%, but R&D up 63% kept the operating loss flat with last year.
- **Neutron delayed**: A tank test failure pushed the first flight to 2026 Q4; full-year free cash flow was -$320M.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Rocket Lab set records in both Q4 revenue and backlog, and the big SDA award proves it can compete with traditional aerospace giants as a prime contractor; but with Neutron repeatedly delayed and $320M burned for the year, a valuation above 60x sales needs Neutron to succeed on schedule.
