## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue doubles, but gross margin drops to 5.6%</h3>
<p>Q4 revenue was NT$720.9B, up 143% YoY, and full-year revenue NT$2.19T, up 108%, with growth coming almost entirely from AI servers. But Q4 gross margin was 5.62%, 1.77pp below Q3 and 2.47pp below a year earlier: full racks book the cost of high-priced components such as GPUs and memory into revenue, so gross profit grows while the ratio is diluted. An opex ratio down to 2.09% offset part of this, but operating margin still fell from 4.78% to 3.53%.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Q4 inventory ships and turns into cash; operating cash flow turns positive</h3>
<p>Inventory piled up to NT$425.3B at the end of Q3 and fell to NT$355.5B in Q4, with inventory days dropping from about 74 to 48; Q4 operating cash flow was +NT$70.8B, free cash flow +NT$59.2B, and cash rose NT$67.0B. But for the full year, component stocking and higher receivables left operating cash flow at -NT$105.8B, and the debt ratio rose from 68.3% to 76.9% — the cost of ramping AI servers.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Full-year net income of NT$57.8B, 53% to minority holders</h3>
<p>Total 2025 net income was NT$57.77B, but only NT$27.41B was attributable to the parent, with NT$30.36B, or 53%, going to non-controlling interests. A sizable share of Wistron's AI server profit sits in subsidiaries it does not wholly own (such as Wiwynn), so parent EPS grew 48% YoY, far less than the doubling in revenue. Investors in Wistron should look at parent-attributable profit and EPS, not consolidated revenue.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: High-double-digit revenue growth expected in 2026, capex raised to NT$60B</h3>
<p>Chairman Simon Lin said at the 3/13 earnings conference that the AI server outlook is positive and expects full-year 2026 revenue to grow by a <b>"high-double-digit percentage"</b>; 2026 capex was raised to NT$60B, mainly to expand capacity in Taiwan and the US (media reports). President Jeff Lin said Q1 order visibility is good and should show significant YoY growth, while Q3 brings a transition between old and new products and some uncertainty remains; the next-generation Vera Rubin uses a cableless design, which means fewer assembly problems and should improve yields.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Targeting small and mid-sized NeoClouds; networking shipments seen up 10x</h3>
<p>Jeff Lin said NeoClouds are emerging in large numbers, and Wistron can deliver up to L11 full racks, targeting small and mid-sized NeoClouds not covered by its existing large customers; these end customers <b>"won't be fighting with our customers"</b>. The division of labor with Wiwynn: Wiwynn handles large CSPs, Wistron the small and mid-sized NeoClouds. Networking shipments are expected to grow 10x in 2026, mainly from its largest North American networking customer, with margins better than PCs and AI systems. Notebooks are hit by memory shortages, with full-year shipments expected to fall 5–10% YoY.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and 2026 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: one-stop from GPU baseboards to full racks</h3>
<p>Wistron makes components such as GPU baseboards as well as L10–L11 system assembly; the component business grew strongly in 2025, and system-level products began shipping in 2025 Q3. Capacity in Taiwan, the US and elsewhere lets it supply customers locally. The technical barrier for ODMs is not high; the real barriers are keeping pace with NVIDIA's annual product cadence in mass production and having the capital to carry large component inventories.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Highly leveraged stocking</b>: Debt ratio of 76.9%, short-term borrowings of NT$235.2B and full-year free cash flow of -NT$145.4B; the faster it expands, the greater the funding pressure.</li>
<li><b>Profit leakage</b>: 53% of full-year net income goes to minority holders, so parent EPS grows far less than consolidated revenue.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Margin dilution</b>: Q4 gross margin was 5.62%, down 1.77pp QoQ, and will be pushed lower still when component price increases are fully passed through.</li>
<li><b>Product transition</b>: Management itself flagged uncertainty from the old-to-new platform transition in 2026 Q3.</li>
<li><b>Equity dilution</b>: A NT$27.1B cash capital increase in 2025 and NT$32.5B of new bonds in one quarter; diluted EPS of NT$8.40 is 7% below basic EPS.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Better inventory turnover</b>: Q4 inventory days fell from about 74 to 48 as stocked components began turning into revenue and cash.</li>
<li><b>New networking business</b>: Shipments seen up 10x with higher margins, which could lift overall margins slightly.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue doubles**: 2025 revenue of NT$2.19T (+108%) and Q4 revenue of NT$720.9B (+143%), led by AI servers.
- **Record EPS, but slower growth**: Full-year EPS of NT$9.04 (+48%), with 53% of net income going to minority holders.
- **Margins diluted**: Q4 gross margin of 5.62% and operating margin of 3.53%, as pass-through costs on full racks push the ratios down.
- **Seasonal cash flow turnaround**: Q4 operating cash flow of +NT$70.8B, but full-year free cash flow of -NT$145.4B and a 76.9% debt ratio.
- **High-double-digit growth expected in 2026**: Capex raised to NT$60B; the Q3 platform transition is the main variable.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Wistron doubled revenue in 2025, but profit was eaten away by low gross margins, minority holders and interest; in 2026 the question is whether NT$60B of expansion brings a higher operating margin, not just bigger revenue.
