## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Revenue doubles and operating leverage begins to show</h3>
<p>Q2 revenue was $2.58B, up 112% YoY and 24% QoQ. Co-founder and CEO Michael Intrator said the company <b>reached an important inflection point this quarter, with scale starting to turn into operating leverage</b>. The operating loss narrowed from $144M in Q1 to $49M, though it was still worse than the profit a year earlier.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: $104B backlog, plus over $25B signed in early Q3</h3>
<p>Revenue backlog at quarter-end was about $104B, and more than $25B of new customer commitments were added in early Q3 (not included). Customers expanded to companies such as Caterpillar, Bentley and Grammarly, while existing customers such as Databricks and Cognition broadened their work with CoreWeave. Intrator said customer demand <b>is accelerating</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Doubled interest expense eats the profit</h3>
<p>Net interest expense was $640M, up 140% YoY and far larger than adjusted operating income of $128M, widening the GAAP net loss to $626M. The adjusted operating margin fell from 16% to 5% because depreciation rises quickly as new capacity comes online, while revenue recognition catches up later.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: $6.4B capex funded by debt and equity investment</h3>
<p>Q2 capex was $6.42B and first-half capex $14.1B, while active power rose nearly 500 MW QoQ to 1.5 GW. During the quarter the company issued more than $10B of unsecured and convertible notes (including its first euro bond), closed a $3.1B delayed-draw term loan and received a $1B strategic investment from Jane Street.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: First to validate Vera Rubin NVL72</h3>
<p>CoreWeave brought up and validated the industry's first NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 system and launched multi-cloud tools such as cross-cloud interconnect and SUNK Anywhere, as well as ARIA, an AI agent research tool. In MLPerf tests it set training and inference records on the Grace Blackwell platform.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: an AI-specialized cloud and first access to new NVIDIA products</h3>
<p>CoreWeave builds its cloud specifically for AI, is often the first provider to bring new NVIDIA platforms online and locks in AI labs and big tech companies with long-term contracts. Its weakness is the capital structure: expansion relies almost entirely on debt and leases, and interest expense will weigh on GAAP earnings for a long time.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>

<li><b>Deeply negative free cash flow</b>: about -$5.7B in Q2, so expansion depends on continuous financing.</li>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>

<li><b>Delivery risk</b>: backlog depends on delivery and service availability, so power and equipment delays would push revenue out.</li>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue doubles**: Q2 revenue of $2.58B (+112%) and adjusted EBITDA of $1.51B, a 59% margin.
- **Huge backlog**: about $104B, with more than $25B of customer commitments added in early Q3.
- **Interest pressure**: interest expense of $640M, up 140% YoY, and a GAAP net loss of $626M.
- **Burning cash to expand**: Q2 capex of $6.4B, free cash flow of about -$5.7B and total debt of $35.1B.
- **Technology lead**: first to validate Vera Rubin NVL72, with active power reaching 1.5 GW.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: CoreWeave doubled Q2 revenue and its backlog topped $100B, showing clear demand for AI cloud; but expansion relies almost entirely on debt, interest and depreciation keep GAAP in the red, and the stock is extremely sensitive to funding costs and execution risk.
