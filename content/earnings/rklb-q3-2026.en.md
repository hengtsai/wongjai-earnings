## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Backlog jumps $1.2B in a quarter; Neutron chooses "higher price, not more volume"</h3>
<p>In Q3 the company signed $1B of new launch contracts and $470M of Space Systems orders. Neutron signed 8 launches worth about <b>$515M</b>, several at the new <b>$70M</b> base price. Spice stressed that <b>Neutron's price is not going to be $70M, it already is $70M</b>, and said the company could actually have signed more this quarter but chose higher prices and fewer launches. CEO Peter Beck said the first 16 Neutron launches are sold out, with the next opening in the 2030s.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Neutron's first flight slips to 2027 after voids found in a tank dome</h3>
<p>The 10-Q disclosed that in October, while building the first-stage composite structure, voids were found in a propellant tank dome; the forward dome must be rebuilt and pass hydrostatic and cryogenic testing. On the call Beck explained that <b>after finding voids in the forward dome of the propellant tank, the company moved the first flight to 2027</b>; the team is racing to build replacement parts but gave no specific month. Spice also disclosed a forward loss provision on the second Neutron vehicle. At the time of our Q4 2025 report the target was still a 2026 Q4 first flight—$515M of new orders are riding on a rocket that has not yet flown.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: Iridium deal is fully funded, but the stock is below the exchange-ratio floor</h3>
<p>The Iridium acquisition carries an enterprise value of about $8B and consideration of $54 per share ($27 in cash plus stock), requiring more than $3B of cash. After completing the ATM in Q3, the $3.6B bridge loan was terminated. Beck said <b>the deal is fully financed and the company does not need to raise more money to complete it</b>. It still awaits FCC and other regulatory approvals and is expected to close in 2027. Note the exchange terms: when RKLB's average price is below $67.50 the exchange ratio is fixed at 0.4; using the 11/10 close of $62.71 as a rough estimate (the actual terms use a volume-weighted average price), consideration is about $52.1 per share, below the nominal $54.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Gross margin down 3pp QoQ, but better than the company itself expected</h3>
<p>Non-GAAP gross margin fell from 41.5% in Q2 to 38.4%, still above the top of guidance at 37%. Spice cited two factors: dilution from <b>the first full quarter after consolidating Mynaric</b>, and <b>a tariff refund in Q2</b> that did not recur, and said the decline was smaller than the company expected. Launch gross margin fell from 42.9% to 35.3%, and revenue per launch dropped to $7.8M (customer mix). Space Systems is now 80% of revenue, making the business look more and more like a satellite manufacturer. Q4 guidance has non-GAAP gross margin recovering to 39–41% on a better mix of launch and Space Systems.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: Cash flow turns positive for the quarter, but profitability moves out with Neutron</h3>
<p>Operating cash flow of +$7.2M came from prepayments, not profits—adjusted EBITDA was still a loss of $18M. Spice said plainly that <b>Neutron spending will stay elevated until the first flight</b> and expects adjusted EBITDA to turn positive within 6 months of the first successful test flight; with the first flight now in 2027, breakeven lands in 2027 at the earliest. Q3 R&D expenses were $98.4M (+39%). For 2027 Spice said only that growth should be better in many respects, without giving numbers.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q4 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: end-to-end "rockets + satellites + components," with its own constellation next</h3>
<p>Electron has flown 99 times with 96% reliability, and Beck said the 100th launch is expected in about a week; the line produces one rocket every 8 days. Space Systems has used a string of acquisitions (SolAero, GEOST, Mynaric, Motiv) to bring solar panels, laser communications and optical payloads in-house. Beck plans for Neutron capacity to be <b>half for all customers and half kept for the company itself</b>—if Iridium comes in, Rocket Lab goes from supplier to an operator that owns spectrum and a constellation.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Neutron slips again</b>: The first flight moved from 2026 Q4 to 2027 with no specific month, and the 10-Q says the schedule could still be affected; revenue recognition for all 16 signed launches shifts later.</li>
<li><b>Iridium integration and regulation</b>: The $8B enterprise-value deal still awaits FCC and foreign regulatory approval, and after closing the company must guarantee about $1.8B of Iridium's term loans.</li>
<li><b>Extreme valuation</b>: Market cap is about $42.7B against TTM revenue of only $882M, a price/sales ratio of about 48x, leaving very little room for error.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Ongoing dilution</b>: Q3 issued 36.76M new shares and weighted shares rose 23% YoY; the stock portion of the Iridium consideration will add more.</li>
<li><b>Gross margin mix pressure</b>: Acquired businesses carry lower margins and revenue per launch fell to $7.8M; non-GAAP gross margin has fallen two quarters in a row from 43.0% in Q1 to 38.4%.</li>
<li><b>Government budget timing</b>: Space Development Agency (SDA) and Space Force contracts make up a large share, so budget delays would affect milestone payments and recognition.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Ample firepower</b>: $4.7B in cash and investments with almost no financial debt; Q4 net interest income of about $45M can offset most of the operating loss.</li>
<li><b>High backlog visibility</b>: The $3.57B backlog is about 4 times TTM revenue, with $1.18B to be recognized within 12 months.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Revenue and backlog both at records**: Q3 revenue of $268M (+73%) beat the top of guidance, and backlog rose $1.2B in the quarter to $3.57B.
- **Neutron is the biggest variable**: The first flight slipped to 2027, but 8 new contracts were signed at the $70M base price, so demand is unaffected.
- **One-time factors behind the wider loss**: The $79.7M net loss includes $48.6M of Iridium-related costs, while the adjusted EBITDA loss actually fell 31% YoY.
- **Iridium funding in place**: The ATM raised $2.4B and the bridge loan was cancelled; the deal awaits FCC approval and is expected to close in 2027.
- **Q4 set for another record**: Revenue guidance is $270–290M, full-year revenue is estimated at about $980M, and non-GAAP gross margin recovers to 39–41%.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Rocket Lab has proven demand and financial strength with its backlog and cash, but justifying a $42.7B market cap still depends on Neutron actually flying in 2027 and Iridium being integrated smoothly.
