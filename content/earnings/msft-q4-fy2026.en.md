## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Azure's 43% beats guidance; new capacity sells out within the quarter</h3>
<p>Azure grew 43% YoY, above the 39–40% guidance given last quarter, and full-year revenue topped $100B for the first time, up 41%. CFO Amy Hood said: <b>"Customer demand continues to exceed available capacity."</b> With CPU and GPU efficiency gains and new capacity delivered early, <b>"That additional in-quarter capacity for Azure was quickly monetized."</b> After GitHub Copilot moved to usage-based billing in June, usage also beat expectations. Q1 FY27 Azure guidance is about 45% (constant currency), with acceleration expected to continue through the first half of the fiscal year.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Longer useful lives; calendar-year capex now about $175B</h3>
<p>Q4 capex was $41B and will exceed $50B in Q1 FY27. Starting in FY27, the estimated useful life of data centers and office buildings is extended from 15 to 25 years, and more data center leases will shift from finance leases to operating leases, which are not counted in capex. Hood said that apart from this the 2026 calendar-year investment plan is unchanged, but on the new basis it is now expected at about $175B (versus about $190B said last quarter). The change gives only a slight benefit to FY27 operating income.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: RPO of $678B, still up 25% excluding OpenAI</h3>
<p>Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) was $678B, up 84% YoY, or 25% excluding OpenAI; about 30% will be recognized within 12 months, up 37% YoY. Hood stressed: <b>"All sequential commercial RPO growth was driven by commitments from customers outside of frontier model companies."</b> Nearly 90% of full-year cloud revenue also came from those customers. Commercial bookings excluding OpenAI grew 18% YoY, versus only 10% including OpenAI, showing how large-customer contracts make bookings swing sharply.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Copilot passes 30M seats; pricing shifts to seats plus usage</h3>
<p>CEO Satya Nadella said paid M365 Copilot seats exceed 30M, with net seat adds more than doubling QoQ; customers with over 50,000 seats grew more than 7x YoY, and NHS England deployed it to 505,000 people. The new E7 suite sold millions of seats in its first two months. He said: <b>"we are also evolving our business model beyond per-seat to per-seat-plus-consumption"</b>, with Cowork adding usage-based billing from July. GitHub Copilot has 50M users, and its revenue grew more than 60% QoQ.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: One-time items lift EPS; consumer still a drag</h3>
<p>Compared with April guidance, one-time items added $0.27 to EPS: a $3.2B gain on the Anthropic investment and lower-than-expected voluntary retirement program costs, net of Xbox severance and impairments. The $4.74 excluding OpenAI still includes the Anthropic gain. More Personal Computing revenue fell 4% YoY, with Windows OEM -5% and Xbox content -10%, and the segment's operating margin fell to 21%. Nadella said Xbox is being restructured with the goal of returning to growth in FY27.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Competitive Moat and Q1 Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core moat: its own compute plus a swappable model system</h3>
<p>Microsoft added 88 data centers this fiscal year and another 1GW of capacity in Q4, roughly doubling total capacity within two years. Its in-house Maia 200 delivers 30% better performance per dollar than the latest hardware in its fleet. Nadella stressed decoupling interfaces, memory and tools from any single model, because <b>"every model is substitutable"</b>; Foundry offers more than 11,000 models to 100,000 customers. Enterprises run everything from Copilot and databases to Agent 365 governance on one platform, so switching costs are high.</p>
</div>

## 4. ⚠️ Risks

<h3 class="risk-red">🔴 High Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rh"><ul>
<li><b>Capex eats cash flow</b>: Q4 cash capex rose 110% YoY and free cash flow fell 23% YoY, and Q1 capex will climb further to over $50B.</li>
<li><b>Depreciation and component costs</b>: Two-thirds of capex is short-lived GPUs and CPUs, higher component prices are raising costs, and gross margin is already down 1.4pp YoY.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-gold">🟡 Medium Risk</h3>
<div class="sb rm"><ul>
<li><b>Capex definition change</b>: With leases reclassified as operating leases, the capex figure shrinks but the rent commitments remain; be careful comparing periods.</li>
<li><b>Customer concentration</b>: RPO rose 84% YoY including OpenAI but only 25% excluding it, so changes in a single customer's contracts amplify swings.</li>
<li><b>Weak PC and Xbox</b>: Windows OEM and devices are expected to fall more than 20% in Q1, as higher component prices dampen PC demand.</li>
</ul></div>

<h3 class="risk-green">🟢 Low Risk / Opportunity</h3>
<div class="sb rl"><ul>
<li><b>Azure re-accelerating</b>: Q1 guidance is about 45%, the company expects acceleration through the first half of the fiscal year, and capacity freed up by efficiency gains can be monetized within the quarter.</li>
<li><b>Usage billing expands the market</b>: Copilot, GitHub and Dynamics all add usage-based billing, leaving room for revenue per seat to rise.</li>
</ul></div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Azure beat**: Up 43% YoY, above the 39–40% guidance, topping $100B for the year, with Q1 guided to about 45%.
- **Earnings include one-offs**: Revenue of $90B (+18%), a 45.1% operating margin, and EPS of $4.74 including about $0.27 of one-time gains.
- **Capex definition change**: Useful lives extended to 25 years; the calendar-year estimate is now about $175B, with Q1 still over $50B.
- **High order visibility**: RPO of $678B (+84%), still up 25% YoY excluding OpenAI.
- **Copilot scaling**: Paid seats exceed 30M, with pricing expanding from per-seat to seats plus usage.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Microsoft closed FY26 with better-than-expected Azure and more than 30M Copilot seats, but free cash flow has kept declining; after the capex definition change, watch lease commitments, not just the capex figure.
