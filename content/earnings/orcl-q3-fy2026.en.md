## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: First time in 15 years that revenue and EPS both grew more than 20%</h3>
<p>The company said this was <b>its first quarter in more than 15 years in which both organic total revenue and non-GAAP EPS grew more than 20% in US dollars</b>. Revenue was $17.19B, up 22% YoY, and non-GAAP EPS was $1.79, up 21%. Cloud was $8.91B, up 44% YoY and now 52% of revenue; traditional software grew only 3%.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: OCI up 84% YoY, accelerating every quarter</h3>
<p>Cloud infrastructure revenue was $4.89B, up 84% YoY, accelerating from $3.35B in Q1 and $4.08B in Q2. Oracle cloud database grew 35% YoY, and multicloud database offered on other clouds grew 531%. The company said cloud demand for AI training and inference <b>continues to grow faster than supply</b>. Cloud and software costs rose 66% YoY, reflecting investment in GPUs and data centers.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: RPO of $553B, with big customers bringing their own funding</h3>
<p>RPO was $553B, up 325% YoY and $29B QoQ. The company said the increase came mainly from large AI contracts, with equipment largely funded by customer prepayments that let Oracle buy GPUs, or by customers buying GPUs themselves and handing them to Oracle, so <b>it does not expect to need additional funding for these contracts</b>. The company also said the finances of some of its largest AI cloud customers have strengthened significantly of late.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: $30B raised; 2026 bond issuance to stay within this limit</h3>
<p>In February Oracle announced plans to raise up to $50B in debt and equity, and said it does not expect to issue additional bonds beyond this amount in calendar 2026; within days it raised $30B through investment-grade bonds and mandatory convertible preferred stock, heavily oversubscribed, while the at-the-market equity portion has not yet started. Total debt rose $42.0B in nine months to $134.6B, and interest expense rose 32% YoY.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: AI coding shrinks teams; FY27 revenue guidance raised to $90B</h3>
<p>The company said AI code generation has become efficient enough for Oracle to reorganize product development into <b>smaller, more agile and more productive groups</b>, building more SaaS with fewer people. FY26 guidance of $67B in revenue and $50B in capex is unchanged, FY27 revenue guidance was raised to $90B, and the company said it can <b>comfortably meet and potentially exceed</b> its growth forecasts beyond FY27.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Twin 20%+ growth**: Revenue of $17.2B (+22%) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.79 (+21%), a first in 15 years.
- **OCI accelerating**: Cloud infrastructure at $4.9B, up 84% YoY, accelerating every quarter.
- **Backlog explosion**: RPO of $553B, up 325% YoY; the company says big customers are funding GPUs themselves.
- **Heavy borrowing**: TTM capex of $48.3B, free cash flow of -$24.7B and total debt of $134.6B.
- **Guidance raised**: FY27 revenue guidance raised from the prior level to $90B.

> 💡 **Bottom line**: Oracle's 84% OCI growth and $553B RPO prove the AI cloud orders are real, but -$24.7B of free cash flow and $134.6B of debt make this transformation highly dependent on capital-market support.
