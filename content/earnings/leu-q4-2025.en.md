## 2. 🔍 Five Key Insights and Management Commentary

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 1: Q4 Earnings Drop as Uranium Sales Plunge While SWU Holds Up</h3>
<p>Q4 revenue fell 3.6% YoY to $146M: SWU revenue rose 128% to $111M, but uranium revenue dropped from $73.2M to $13.4M. Gross margin declined from 40.8% to 23.9%, operating income fell 72% YoY to $12.8M, and $17.8M in net income benefited from $16.5M of investment income.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 2: Full-Year SWU Volume Up 23%, Lifting LEU Gross Profit 19%</h3>
<p>Full-year LEU segment revenue dipped 1% to $346M, yet a 23% jump in SWU volume and a 13% drop in average unit cost—despite a 1% dip in average price—lifted segment gross profit 19% to $112M. CEO Amir Vexler noted that <b>the steep rise in the LEU price curve continues to confirm a clear market need for additional enrichment capacity</b>.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 3: HALEU Contract Margin Compression</h3>
<p>Technical Solutions full-year revenue grew 11% to $103M, but gross profit fell from $17.6M to $6.0M, with an estimated Q4 gross loss of $2.4M. The drag came from extending Phase 2 of the HALEU operations contract to 2026/1/31, with costs during the extension still under negotiation and unrecognized. Centrus produced over 1 metric ton of HALEU UF6 in 2025.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 4: Expansion Underway as DOE Selects Centrus for $900M HALEU Award</h3>
<p>Centrus began manufacturing centrifuges in-house in Piketon, Ohio in December 2025, and on 2026/1/5 the DOE selected its subsidiary for a $900M HALEU expansion task order (still under negotiation). Vexler said the backlog plus the planned 12 metric tons of HALEU capacity <b>should allow the company to achieve its nth-of-a-kind cost milestone</b>. Before the market opened on 2/11, Centrus named Fluor as EPC contractor.</p>
</div>

<div class="sb">
<h3>🎯 Insight 5: $3.8B Backlog, Flat 2026 Revenue Outlook, Capital Deployment Surges</h3>
<p>Year-end backlog stood at $3.8B extending to 2040: ~$2.9B in LEU (including ~$2.3B of contingent orders dependent on new capacity) and ~$900M in Technical Solutions. 2026 revenue guidance of $425M–$475M is roughly flat vs. 2025, while capital deployment of $350M–$500M jumps sharply from $19.7M CapEx in 2025.</p>
</div>

## 3. 🛡️ Moat and 2026 Guidance Outlook

<div class="sb">
<h3>🏰 Core Moat: One of Few Enrichers Ready for Immediate National-Security Production</h3>
<p>Centrus produced over 1 metric ton of HALEU in 2025, and the Fluor release called it the only immediately deployable option for national-security missions; the NNSA also notified its intent to sole-source certain enrichment services. The weakness is that expansion demands substantial public and private capital, so fulfilling contingent backlog depends on financing and construction execution.</p>
</div>

## 6. 📋 Key Takeaways

- **Q4 Softness**: Revenue $146M (-3.6%), net income $17.8M (-67%), gross margin down from 40.8% to 23.9%.
- **SWU Drives Core Results**: Full-year SWU revenue +21%, volume +23%, lifting LEU segment gross profit +19%.
- **HALEU Contract Drag**: Technical Solutions full-year gross profit fell 66%, with extension costs still unpriced.
- **Capital Secured**: Equity and convertible offerings raised ~$1.3B, bringing cash to $1.96B and net cash to ~$780M.
- **Expansion Begins**: DOE selected Centrus for a $900M HALEU task order; 2026 capital deployment $350M–$500M on flat revenue guidance.

> 💡 **One-Line Takeaway**: Centrus leaned on SWU volume growth to support 2025 profits and raised equity and convertibles to build $1.96B in cash as expansion begins, yet a sharp Q4 gross margin drop, flat 2026 revenue guidance, and surging capital spending sent shares down 20.7% on day one.
